# H.R. 11025

> To amend the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 to entitle eligible employees to take neonatal intensive care leave.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Stand By NICU Babies and Families Act”.

## § 2. Findings

Congress finds the following:

- **(1)** Across the United States, dire maternal and infant health outcomes continue to endanger growing families, placing a significant burden on working parents as they navigate postpartum recovery, newborn care, and job security.
- **(2)** Preterm birth and low birth weight are among the leading causes of infant death in the United States. In 2024, the preterm birth rate in the United States was 10.4 percent, with prematurity as a leading cause of admission to the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) followed by low birth weight and other serious health conditions requiring specialized care. NICU facilities are equipped with advanced technology and highly trained medical professionals who deliver comprehensive care to the most vulnerable newborns.
- **(3)** The NICU environment can be stressful for babies and their parents. Preterm infants are often exposed to loud noises and bright lights, in addition to regular and painful medical interventions.
- **(4)** When a baby is born prematurely, parents often experience significant physical, mental, and emotional strain as they adjust to their new normal. Two-thirds of maternal deaths in the United States occur during the postpartum period. The demands of caring for a NICU infant frequently lead parents to deprioritize their own postpartum health, further worsening maternal health outcomes nationwide.
- **(5)** Mothers with infants in the NICU face a higher risk of anxiety and postpartum depression compared to mothers of babies that are not in the NICU. Fathers with infants in the NICU are also more likely to experience greater stress and anxiety than fathers of non-NICU babies.
- **(6)** During such a challenging time for families, parental presence and care in the NICU can make a substantial difference in improving neonatal health outcomes. Parental involvement in the NICU, particularly through skin-to-skin care, is associated with reduced acute pain responses, better weight gain, enhanced growth and development, lower risk of hypothermia, earlier hospital discharge, improved cognitive outcomes in childhood, and strengthened parent-child bonding and nurturing interactions.
- **(7)** The Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (FMLA) (29 U.S.C. 2601 et seq.) is the only Federal law in the United States that provides eligible workers with guaranteed workplace leave to take care of themselves and their families. For working parents, the FMLA provides essential time to bond with a newborn while protecting their employment.
- **(8)** While NICU parents are eligible to use the FMLA to care for and bond with their seriously ill baby, the FMLA does not adequately address the unique needs and experiences of parents with a baby in the NICU. Working parents with a baby in the NICU can easily exhaust most or all of their allotted FMLA leave during their infant’s hospitalization, leaving little to no time available for bonding once the baby goes home.
- **(9)** Parental presence in the NICU is often hindered by the competing demands of work and family outside of the NICU, reducing critical familial bonding and care time with their preterm infant during a period when parental engagement is essential to newborn development.
- **(10)** When parents with a baby in the NICU return to work in order to conserve their FMLA bonding leave for when the baby returns home, they are forfeiting crucial time with their new baby at a pivotal time for both NICU babies and NICU parents.
- **(11)** This Act seeks to support the needs of America’s most vulnerable infants and their families, by providing up to 12 additional weeks of FMLA leave for an eligible worker for the period when a baby is in the NICU.

## § 3. Neonatal intensive care leave

- **(a)** *Neonatal intensive care leave requirement* Section 102(a) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2612(a)) is amended—
  - **(1)** by redesignating paragraph (5) as paragraph (6); and
  - **(2)** by inserting after paragraph (4) the following:
    > - **(5)** *Neonatal intensive care leave*
    >   - **(A)** *Entitlement* Subject to section 103 and subsection (d)(3), an eligible employee shall be entitled to a total of 12 workweeks of leave during a single 12-month period because a son or daughter of such eligible employee is receiving care in a neonatal intensive care unit for a serious health condition.
    >   - **(B)** *Combined leave total* Subject to subsection (d)(3), during the single 12-month period described in subparagraph (A), an eligible employee shall be entitled to a combined total of 24 workweeks of leave under paragraph (1) and subparagraph (A) of this paragraph. Nothing in this paragraph shall be construed to limit the availability of leave under paragraph (1) or (3) during any other 12-month period.
    >   - **(C)** *Relationship to other family and medical leave* During the single 12-month period described in subparagraph (A) of this paragraph, with respect to any leave that an eligible employee is entitled to take under subparagraph (A), (B), or (C) of paragraph (1), and under subparagraph (A) of this paragraph, such leave taken by the employee—
    >     - **(i)** shall be designated and counted as leave taken under subparagraph (A) of this paragraph; and
    >     - **(ii)** may not be designated or counted as leave taken under subparagraph (A), (B), or (C) of paragraph (1).
    >   - **(D)** *Expiration of entitlement* The entitlement to leave under subparagraph (A) of this paragraph shall expire at the end of the 12-month period beginning on the date of birth of the son or daughter receiving care in a neonatal intensive care unit for a serious health condition.

- **(b)** *Servicemember family leave combined leave total* Section 102(a)(4) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2612(a)(4)) is amended—
  - **(1)** in the first sentence, by striking “paragraphs (1) and (3)” and inserting “paragraphs (1), (3), and (5)(A)”; and
  - **(2)** by second sentence, by striking “paragraph (1)” and inserting “paragraph (1) or (5)(A)”.
- **(c)** *Leave taken intermittently or on a reduced leave schedule* Section 102(b)(1) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2612(b)(1)) is amended by striking “subsection (a)(1)(E)” and inserting “subsections (a)(1)(E) and (a)(5)(A)”.
- **(d)** *Relationship to paid leave* Section 102(d) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2612(d)) is amended—
  - **(1)** by amending paragraph (1) to read as follows:
    > - **(1)** *Unpaid leave* If an employer provides paid leave for fewer than the total number of workweeks to which an eligible employee is entitled under subsection (a), the additional workweeks of leave necessary to attain the total number of workweeks of leave to which the employee is entitled may be provided without compensation.

  - **(2)** in paragraph (2), by adding at the end the following:
    > - **(C)** *Neonatal intensive care leave* An eligible employee may elect to substitute any of the accrued paid vacation leave, personal leave, family leave, or medical or sick leave of the employee for leave provided under subsection (a)(5)(A) for any part of the 12-week period of such leave under such subsection, except that nothing in this title requires an employer to provide paid sick leave or paid medical leave in any situation in which the employer would not normally provide any such paid leave.

- **(e)** *Certification* Section 103(a) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2613(a)) is amended by striking “paragraph (1) or paragraph (3) of section 102(a)” and inserting “paragraph (1), (3), or (5)(A) of section 102(a)”.
- **(f)** *Employment and benefits protection* Section 104(c) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2614(c)) is amended—
  - **(1)** in paragraph (2)(B)(i), by striking “or under section 102(a)(3)” and inserting “, under section 102(a)(3), or under section 102(a)(5)(A)”; and
  - **(2)** in paragraph (3)(A)(i), by inserting “or 102(a)(5)(A)” after “section 102(a)(1)(C)”.
- **(g)** *Civil action by employees* Section 107(a)(1)(A)(i)(II) of the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (29 U.S.C. 2617(a)(1)(A)(i)(II)) is amended by striking “a sum equal to 12 weeks (or 26 weeks, in a case involving leave under section 102(a)(3))” and inserting “a sum equal to 12 weeks (or 24 weeks or 26 weeks, in a case involving leave under section 102(a)(5)(A) or leave under section 102(a)(3), respectively)”.
