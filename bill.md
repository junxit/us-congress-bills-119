# S. 5527

> To amend the Immigration and Nationality Act to provide for the inadmissibility of certain aliens seeking citizenship for children by giving birth in the United States, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title; table of contents

## § 2. Findings; sense of Congress

- **(a)** *Findings* Congress finds the following:
  - **(1)** Birth tourism is the practice of foreign nationals traveling to the United States for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth in the United States.
  - **(2)** The Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs of the Senate has determined—
    - **(A)** changes made to Department of State policies in 2015 made birth tourism more accessible and more difficult to investigate and prosecute;
    - **(B)** birth tourists—
      - **(i)** frequently make repeat trips to the United States and declare substantial amounts of cash upon entry; and
      - **(ii)** sometimes leave unpaid medical expenses that are ultimately borne by United States taxpayers; and
    - **(C)** commercial birth tourism companies have organized and promoted travel packages designed to facilitate birth tourism in the United States.
  - **(3)** The Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs of the Senate has recommended that Congress amend the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1101 et seq.) to clarify that the Act does not authorize the issuance of visas to temporary visitors for pleasure whose primary purpose of travel is to obtain United States citizenship for a child.
  - **(4)** In 2020, the Department of State amended its regulations to clarify that travel to the United States for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth in the United States is not a permissible basis for the issuance of a B nonimmigrant visitor visa.
  - **(5)** The Department of State has determined that the Immigration and Nationality Act's authorization for temporary visitors for pleasure does not extend to individuals whose primary purpose of travel is to obtain United States citizenship for a child through birth tourism.
  - **(6)** The Department of State has continued to identify, investigate, and dismantle organized birth tourism networks operating overseas, including networks that used fraudulent documents, visa facilitators, and coached applicants to evade immigration screening.
- **(b)** *Sense of Congress* It is the sense of Congress that preserving the integrity of the United States immigration system and ensuring that temporary visitor programs are used only for their intended purposes are essential to maintaining public confidence in the rule of law and the value of American citizenship.

## § 3. Inadmissibility of aliens seeking citizenship for children by giving birth in the United States

- **(a)** *In general* Section 212(a)(10) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(10)) is amended by adding at the end the following:
  > - **(F)** *Aliens seeking citizenship for children by giving birth in the United States*
  >   - **(i)** *In general* Any alien seeking admission to the United States as a nonimmigrant under section 101(a)(15)(B) for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth to such child in the United States is inadmissible.
  >   - **(ii)** *Rules of construction* Nothing in this subparagraph may be construed to render inadmissible—
  >     - **(I)** an alien seeking legitimate medical treatment relating to childbirth if obtaining citizenship for the child is not the primary purpose for seeking admission; or
  >     - **(II)** an alien if the child she is carrying is the biological offspring of a United States citizen, a United States lawful permanent resident, or a person who has been granted permanent resident status in the United States as a refugee or asylee.

- **(b)** *Denial of admission* Section 212(d) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1182(d)) is amended by inserting after paragraph (1) the following:
  > - **(2)** A consular officer or a U.S. Customs and Border Protection officer may deny admission to any alien if such officer has a reasonable belief that the primary purpose of such alien's travel is to obtain United States citizenship for a child by giving birth to such child in the United States.

- **(c)** *Prohibition on granting humanitarian parole to aliens engaging in birth tourism* Section 212(d)(5) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1182(d)(5)) is amended—
  - **(1)** by redesignating subparagraph (C) as subparagraph (D); and
  - **(2)** by inserting after subparagraph (B) the following:
    > - **(C)**
    >   - **(i)** Except as provided in clause (ii), the Secretary of Homeland Security may not parole into the United States an alien who is seeking to enter the United States, or to remain in the United States, for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth to such child while in the United States.
    >   - **(ii)** Clause (i) shall not apply to any alien returning pursuant to an advance parole document issued on the basis of a pending application for adjustment of status under section 245.

## § 4. Codification of Department of State rule clarifying eligibility for B–2 tourist visas

Section 212(q) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1182(q)) is amended—

- **(1)** by inserting “(1)” before “Any alien”; and
- **(2)** by adding at the end the following:
  > - **(2)**
  >   - **(A)** For purposes of section 101(a)(15)(B), the term pleasure does not include obtaining a visa for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth in the United States.
  >   - **(B)** Any applicant for a nonimmigrant visa under section 101(a)(15)(B) who seeks to enter the United States to receive medical treatment shall be denied a visa pursuant to section 214(b) if such applicant is unable to establish, to the satisfaction of a consular officer, that—
  >     - **(i)** he or she has a legitimate reason for traveling to the United States for medical treatment;
  >     - **(ii)** a medical practitioner or facility in the United States has agreed to provide such treatment;
  >     - **(iii)** the applicant has reasonably estimated the duration of the visit and all associated costs; and
  >     - **(iv)** he or she has the means derived from lawful sources and intent to pay for the medical treatment and all incidental expenses, including transportation and living expenses, either independently or with the pre-arranged assistance of others.
  >   - **(C)**
  >     - **(i)** Any applicant for a nonimmigrant visa under section 101(a)(15)(B) who a consular officer has reason to believe would give birth to a child during her stay in the United States is presumed to be traveling for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for such child.
  >     - **(ii)** A visa applicant may rebut the presumption under clause (i) by establishing, to the satisfaction of a consular officer, she has a legitimate primary purpose for giving birth in the United States other than obtaining United States citizenship for the child.

## § 5. Prohibition on using the Visa Waiver Program for birth tourism

- **(a)** *Guam and Northern Mariana Islands Visa Waiver Program* Section 212(l) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1182(l)) is amended by adding at the end the following:
  > - **(7)** *Not engaging in birth tourism* No alien may be granted a waiver of subsection (a)(7)(B)(i) for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth to such child while in Guam or in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands.

- **(b)** *Visa Waiver Program* Section 217(a) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1187(a)) is amended by adding at the end the following:
  > - **(13)** *Not engaging in birth tourism* The alien is not applying to enter the United States through the Electronic System for Travel Authorization for the primary purpose of obtaining United States citizenship for a child by giving birth to such child while in the United States.

## § 6. Secondary inspections

Section 235(a)(3) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1225(a)(3)) is amended by adding at the end the following: “An immigration officer who has reason to believe an arriving alien may be inadmissible under section 212(a)(10)(F) shall refer such alien for secondary inspection in accordance with procedures prescribed by the Secretary of Homeland Security.”.

## § 7. Expedited removal for birth tourism

Section 235(b)(1) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. 1225(b)(1)) is amended—

- **(1)** in subparagraph (A)(i), by striking “section 212(a)(6)(C) or 212(a)(7)” and inserting “paragraph (6)(C), (7), or (10)(F) of section 212(a)”; and
- **(2)** by adding at the end the following:
  > - **(H)** *Removal destination for birth tourists* An alien who is inadmissible under section 212(a)(10)(F) may be removed to the country from which the alien traveled to the United States unless such alien voluntarily withdraws his or her application for admission and returns to his or country of citizenship or last habitual residence. This subparagraph does not apply to any alien woman who gives birth in the United States if such birth is incidental to her lawful admission to the United States.

## § 8. Enhancement of criminal penalties for facilitating, sponsoring, organizing, or profiting from birth tourism

Section 1425 of title 18, United States Code, is amended—

- **(1)** in subsection (a), by striking “or” at the end;
- **(2)** in subsection (b), by striking the em dash and inserting “; or”;
- **(3)** by inserting after subsection (b) the following:
  > - **(c)** Whoever, while acting independently or on behalf of an entity, including foreign entity of concern (as defined in section 9901(8) of the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (15 U.S.C. 4651(8))), knowingly facilitates, sponsors, organizes, advertises, profits from, or aids and abets birth tourism (as described in section 2(a)(1) of the Birth Tourism Elimination Act,)

- **(4)** in the undesignated matter following subsection (c), as added by paragraph (3), by inserting “an offense described in subsection (c) or” after “15 years (in the case of”.

## § 9. Reporting requirements

The Secretary of Homeland Security, in consultation with the Secretary of State, shall submit a semiannual report to the Committee on the Judiciary of the Senate, Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs of the Senate, the Committee on Foreign Relations of the Senate, the Committee on the Judiciary of the House of Representatives, and the Committee on Homeland Security of the House of Representatives, and the Committee on Foreign Affairs of the House of Representatives that identifies, with respect to the previous month—

- **(1)** the number of B–2 nonimmigrant visa requests that were denied due to the prohibitions set forth in subsections (a)(10) or (q)(2) of section 212 of the Immigration and Nationality Act, as added by this Act;
- **(2)** the number of aliens who were denied humanitarian parole due to the prohibition set forth in section 212(d)(5)(C) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, as added by section 3(c);
- **(3)** the number of aliens who were denied entry into the United States due to the prohibition set forth in section 217(a)(13) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, as added by section 5;
- **(4)** the number of investigations that were initiated to identify aliens seeking to enter the United States for birth tourism purposes;
- **(5)** the number of prosecutions that were initiated against individuals engaged in birth tourism; and
- **(6)** the number of aliens who were removed from the United States for violating the prohibition against birth tourism.
