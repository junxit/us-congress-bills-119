# H. RES. 1637

> Recognizing the significance of equal pay and the pay disparity between disabled women and nondisabled men.

## Preamble

- Whereas more than 60 years after Congress enacted the Equal Pay Act of 1963 (29 U.S.C. 206 note; Public Law 88–38), an analysis of 2024 data from the Bureau of the Census shows that disabled women workers overall, including those working part-time and seasonally, are paid an average of 56 cents for every dollar paid to nondisabled men, and among full-time, year-round workers are paid 68 cents on the dollar;
- Whereas an analysis of 2024 data from the Bureau of the Census shows that among full-time, year-round workers— (1)for every dollar paid to white, non-Hispanic, nondisabled men— (A) disabled Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander women are paid 73 cents; (B)disabled white, non-Hispanic women are paid 64 cents; (C)disabled American Indian and Alaska Native women are paid 60 cents; (D)disabled multiracial women are paid 60 cents; (E)disabled Black women are paid 57 cents; and (F)disabled Latinas are paid 53 cents; and (2)while disabled people overall experience a wage gap, disabled women, particularly many disabled women of color, face especially large gaps;
- Whereas of the 6 types of disability assessed in the American Community Survey— (1)disabled women workers face a wage gap compared to nondisabled men regardless of type of disability; and (2)disabled women workers, including those working part-time and seasonally, who have difficulty with independent living experience the largest wage gap, typically paid just 39 cents for every dollar paid to nondisabled men in 2024;
- Whereas among all workers, including those working part-time and seasonally, disabled women veterans are paid an average of 66 cents for every dollar paid to nondisabled veteran men in 2024;
- Whereas the wage gap remains large for disabled women with more education, as disabled women with a bachelor’s degree working full-time, year-round in 2024 are typically paid $61,000 per year, just 66 percent of what nondisabled men with the same educational attainment are typically paid, and $4,000 less than nondisabled men with some college education or an associate’s degree;
- Whereas of disabled workers who live in institutional group quarters, including those working part-time or seasonally, disabled women are typically paid just $11,500 per year and disabled men are paid $12,000 per year, while nondisabled men are typically paid an average of $54,000 per year in 2024;
- Whereas disabled women experience occupational segregation and are overrepresented in low-paid health care, clerical, and social service jobs;
- Whereas segregated workplaces and the subminimum wage for disabled employees stifle competitive integrated employment for disabled women;
- Whereas many systemic barriers affect access to livable wages and employment opportunities for disabled women, including— (1)discrimination; (2)public benefits work disincentives; (3)broken health care infrastructure; (4)increased employment-related costs; (5)inadequate vocational rehabilitation services; and (6)a lack of access to supported employment services; and
- Whereas LGBTQI+ disabled people face additional barriers to employment, and more inclusive data on LGBTQI+ disabled workers is needed to determine the added impact of these barriers on wages and workforce participation, particularly for trans and nonbinary disabled people who are often excluded from data: Now, therefore, be it

That the House of Representatives—

- **(1)** recognizes the pay disparity between disabled women and nondisabled men and the impact of that pay disparity on women, families, and the United States; and
- **(2)** reaffirms its commitment to supporting equal pay for disabled women, narrowing the gender, disability, and racial wage gaps and addressing the systemic barriers that drive those inequities.
