# H.R. 10776

> To amend the Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972 to change certain requirements regarding certain allowed take of marine mammals, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Amendments to Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972

- **(a)** *Definitions* Section 3 of the Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972 (16 U.S.C. 1362) is amended—
  - **(1)** in paragraph (18)—
    - **(A)** in subparagraph (A)—
      - **(i)** in clause (i), by striking “has the potential to injure” and inserting “injures”; and
      - **(ii)** in clause (ii), by striking “has the potential to disturb” and inserting “disturbs”; and
    - **(B)** in subparagraph (B)—
      - **(i)** in clause (i), by striking “or has the significant potential to injure”; and
      - **(ii)** in clause (ii), by striking “or is likely to disturb”; and
  - **(2)** by adding at the end the following:
    > - **(30)** The term negligible impact means an impact that is not reasonably likely to significantly and adversely affect the applicable species or stock through an effect on the annual rate of survival of the applicable species or stock.

- **(c)** *Objective application of best scientific and commercial data available*
  - **(1)** *In general* Title I of the Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972 (16 U.S.C. 1371 et seq.) is amended by adding at the end the following:
    > ## § 121. Objective application of best scientific and commercial data available
    >
    > Each decision, determination, and finding made under this title shall be made based solely upon the objective application of the best scientific and commercial data available and without application of precautionary factors or assumptions or any other factors or assumptions that bias the objective application of the best scientific and commercial data available, including each decision, determination, and finding regarding—
    >
    > - **(1)** likely marine mammal distribution or presence;
    > - **(2)** the estimated number of marine mammals in a species or stock;
    > - **(3)** the estimated population trend or status of a marine mammal species or stock;
    > - **(4)** the estimated geographic range of a marine mammal species or stock;
    > - **(5)** the occurrence, extent, quantity, and nature of take of marine mammals; and
    > - **(6)** the likelihood and extent of exposure of marine mammals to human impacts.
