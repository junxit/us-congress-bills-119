# H.R. 10476

> To require coverage of annual glaucoma screenings for certain individuals, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Glaucoma Vision Act of 2026”.

## § 2. Findings

Congress finds the following:

- **(1)** Approximately 4.22 million people in the United States were living with glaucoma in 2022, and about 1.49 million people have vision affecting glaucoma with measurable visual field loss.
- **(2)** Approximately half of people with glaucoma are unaware they have the disease; one NHANES based analysis estimated about 2.4 million Americans with undiagnosed and untreated glaucoma, representing roughly 78 percent of those with definite disease.
- **(3)** The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention projects that more than 3 million Americans currently have glaucoma and that this number will increase to about 6.3 million by 2050 as the population ages.
- **(4)** Vision loss and blindness substantially increase Medicare and overall medical spending, with earlier work showing that vision loss is associated with billions of dollars in additional non-eye-related medical costs.
- **(5)** Adults at higher risk for glaucoma include individuals who are 40 years of age or older and are of African, Hispanic/Latino, or Asian ancestry, as well as individuals with clinical risk factors for glaucoma, including diabetes; a family history of glaucoma; elevated intraocular pressure; very low or very high blood pressure; myopia; a history of corneal refractive surgery or other causes of reduced central corneal thickness; or chronic corticosteroid use. All individuals may also be at higher risk if they have a first-degree relative with diagnosed glaucoma or have uncontrolled diabetes.
- **(6)** Economic burden studies group glaucoma within major adult visual disorders and estimate that vision problems jointly cost tens of billions of dollars annually in direct medical costs, lost productivity, disability benefits, and lost tax revenues.
- **(7)** Medicare part B currently covers one glaucoma screening every 12 months for certain high-risk individuals, including people with diabetes, those with a family history of glaucoma, African Americans age 50 and older, and Hispanics age 65 and older.
- **(8)** By contrast, Medicaid programs, private sector group health plans, and Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) plans are not uniformly required by Federal law to cover glaucoma screening, leaving significant gaps in early detection for many high-risk adults.

## § 4. Glaucoma detection and prevention program

- **(a)** *In general* The Director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, acting through the head of the Vision Health Initiative, (in this section referred to as the “Director”) shall expand and support glaucoma detection and prevention activities, including by awarding grants, on a competitive basis, to eligible entities to provide free or low-cost glaucoma screenings and follow-up treatment to uninsured and underinsured high-risk individuals.
- **(b)** *Applications* To be eligible to receive a grant under this section, an entity shall submit to the Director an application at such time, in such manner, and containing such information as the Director determines appropriate.
- **(c)** *Use of funds* A grant under this section may be used—
  - **(1)** to provide the screenings and treatment referred to in subsection (a); and
  - **(2)** as it relates to such screenings and treatment, for—
    - **(A)** outreach and community education;
    - **(B)** mobile screening units;
    - **(C)** care coordination and linkage to ongoing ophthalmologic care;
    - **(D)** the evaluation described in subsection (e), including data collection to assist in preparing such evaluation; and
    - **(E)** glaucoma disease surveillance.
- **(d)** *Priority* In awarding grants under this section, the Director shall give priority to projects serving racial and ethnic minority communities and other underserved populations with a high burden of glaucoma and vision loss.
- **(e)** *Evaluation* As a condition of receiving a grant under this section, an eligible entity shall submit to the Director a report evaluating the effectiveness of the activities funded by such grant.
- **(f)** *Eligible entity defined* In this section, the term “eligible entity” means any of the following:
  - **(1)** A State or territory of the United States (including the District of Columbia).
  - **(2)** A local health department.
  - **(3)** A community health center.
  - **(4)** An academic medical center.
  - **(5)** A nonprofit organization.
  - **(6)** Such other entity as the Director determines appropriate.
- **(g)** *Authorization of appropriations* There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this section $10,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2028.

## § 5. Glaucoma and optic nerve damage research

Subpart 9 of part C of title IV of the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 285i et seq.) is amended by adding at the end the following:

> ## § 456A. Grants for glaucoma and optic nerve damage research
>
> - **(a)** *In general* The Director of the Institute shall award grants, on a competitive basis, for research related to glaucoma and optic nerve damage, including—
>   - **(1)** optic nerve regeneration and repair;
>   - **(2)** neuroprotection strategies to prevent retinal ganglion cell loss;
>   - **(3)** gene therapies directed at glaucoma-related pathways; and
>   - **(4)** stem cell-based therapies and other advanced approaches to preserving or restoring vision.
> - **(b)** *Authorization of appropriations* There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this section $10,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2028.
