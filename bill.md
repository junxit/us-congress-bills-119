# S. RES. 961

> Expressing the sense of the Senate that the Federal Emergency Management Agency is an essential Federal institution, that disaster response and long-term recovery are critical statutory obligations of the Federal Government, and that robust Federal coordination is required to support States facing catastrophic natural disasters, including devastating wildfires in the western United States.

## Preamble

- Whereas, according to an August 2026 Government Accountability Office assessment, due to the Trump Administration’s use of voluntary separations programs, probationary employee terminations, and restrictions on hiring, more than 4,300 employees separated from the Federal Emergency Management Agency (referred to in this preamble as “FEMA”) in fiscal year 2025 alone, making up about 17 percent of its workforce;
- Whereas, in December 2025, the Trump Administration developed a staffing plan to reduce FEMA's workforce by about half;
- Whereas, in May 2026, the Trump Administration’s FEMA Review Council recommended changes that would shift substantially more disaster response responsibility toward States, including by raising the threshold for qualifying for Federal disaster assistance;
- Whereas, in an October 15, 2026, memorandum to all FEMA employees, the Administrator of FEMA detailed the planned efforts of FEMA to decrease the reliance of States on the Federal Government for disaster assistance;
- Whereas, on October 20, 2026, at a conference held by the National Emergency Management Association, the Administrator of FEMA further detailed the plans of the Trump Administration to significantly reduce Federal disaster aid and fundamentally shift primary disaster recovery burdens onto individual States;
- Whereas FEMA was established to ensure coordinated, well-funded, and rapid Federal assistance to disaster response and recovery efforts that exceed the individual capabilities of State, local, and Tribal governments;
- Whereas catastrophic wildfires in the western United States have significantly grown in frequency, severity, and scale, causing billions of dollars in infrastructure damage, destroying thousands of homes, and threatening entire communities;
- Whereas State, local, and Tribal jurisdictions, despite substantial investments in emergency preparedness, encounter large scale or multi-jurisdictional natural disasters, such as wildfires, that exceed their regional capacity and require unified Federal personnel, equipment, and financial backing;
- Whereas expecting States and localities to independently fund and maintain the massive logistical infrastructure required for major disaster recovery would lead to severe resource depletion in those jurisdictions, delayed emergency responses, and unequal protections for citizens based solely on their State of residence;
- Whereas the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 5121 et seq.) establishes that Federal emergency assistance is an indispensable statutory obligation designed to ensure orderly, reliable Federal assistance to State, local, and Tribal jurisdictions facing catastrophic natural disasters;
- Whereas the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act of 2006 (6 U.S.C. 701 et seq.) expanded the authorities of FEMA, solidifying its role as the lead Federal agency tasked with integrating and coordinating the Federal response to catastrophic natural disasters; and
- Whereas the Sandy Recovery Improvement Act of 2013 (42 U.S.C. 5121 note; Public Law 113–2) and the Disaster Recovery Reform Act of 2018 (Public Law 115–254; 132 Stat. 3438) acknowledged the shared responsibility between Federal, State, and local governments for disaster response and recovery and further strengthened Federal disaster authorities to ensure robust mitigation, streamlined public assistance, and preparedness for the next catastrophic event in the United States: Now, therefore, be it

That it is the sense of the Senate that—

- **(1)** the Federal Emergency Management Agency is vital to national security and public safety and must remain fully funded, staffed, and operational;
- **(2)** any executive branch effort to systematically reduce Federal disaster aid, restrict assistance, or shift primary disaster recovery burdens onto individual States directly undermines and contradicts congressional intent as codified in the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 5121 et seq.);
- **(3)** the Federal Government must honor its statutory obligations to States, localities, Tribes, and the people of the United States under the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 5121 et seq.), the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act of 2006 (6 U.S.C. 701 et seq.), and the Disaster Recovery Reform Act of 2018 (Public Law 115–254; 132 Stat. 3438) to provide personnel, equipment, and financial resources to jurisdictions overwhelmed by catastrophic events;
- **(4)** robust Federal coordination is necessary to support the western United States and other vulnerable regions facing increasingly severe wildfires and other natural disasters; and
- **(5)** the Senate rejects any policy framework that creates unequal protections for citizens of the United States based on geographic residence or State fiscal capacity during times of catastrophic national crisis.
