# H.R. 10909

> To amend the Death in Custody Reporting Act of 2013 to require submission of reports to the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Deaths in Custody Reporting Reform Act”.

## § 2. Death in Custody Reporting Act amendments

The Death in Custody Reporting Act of 2013 (Public Law 113–242) is amended—

- **(1)** by redesignating sections 2 and 3 as sections 3 and 4, respectively;
- **(2)** by adding after section 1 the following:
  > ## § 2. Purposes
  >
  > The purposes of this Act are—
  >
  > - **(1)** to improve the reporting of deaths in custody, including deaths that occur—
  >   - **(A)** in, in transit to, or departing from, jail;
  >   - **(B)** in, in transit to, or departing from, prison;
  >   - **(C)** in, in transit to, or departing from, temporary law enforcement detention;
  >   - **(D)** in, in transit to, or departing from, an immigration detention facility;
  >   - **(E)** in, in transit to, or departing from, a hospital or care facility to which a person in the custody of any facility described in this section was transferred in the 30-day period before death, regardless of whether such person was released from custody before death;
  >   - **(F)** while a person is engaged with law enforcement during an arrest, while a person is pursued by or seeks to elude law enforcement, or while law enforcement is otherwise involved in the death;
  >   - **(G)** in, in transit to, or departing from, a juvenile facility; or
  >   - **(H)** in, in transit to, or departing from, any other custodial or detention facility, including those facilities operated by private entities.
  > - **(2)** to better understand the prevalence and characteristics of deaths that occur in custody;
  > - **(3)** to develop and implement policies, programs, and practices for the identification, prevention, and reduction of deaths in custody;
  > - **(4)** to increase the available data at the Federal, State, and local level, of deaths in custody, thereby improving detention policies, law enforcement activities, and the management and administration of correctional facilities; and
  > - **(5)** to facilitate the standardization of the definitions used when collecting data on the incidence of deaths in custody.

  > ## § 5. Requirements of the Director of the National Institute of Justice relating to death in custody
  >
  > - **(a)** *Study required* The Director of the National Institute of Justice shall carry out a study of the information reported under sections 3(a) and 4(a) to—
  >   - **(1)** determine means by which such information can be used to reduce the number of deaths in custody; and
  >   - **(2)** examine the relationship, if any, between the number of such deaths and the actions of management of such jails, or personnel acting on behalf of such prisons, and other carceral facilities relating to such deaths.
  > - **(b)** *Reports*
  >   - **(1)** *Final report* Not later than 3 years after the date of enactment of this section, and every 3 years thereafter, the Director of the National Institute of Justice shall prepare and submit to Congress a report that contains—
  >     - **(A)** the findings of the study required under subsection (a); and
  >     - **(B)** the information reported under section 6(a).
  >   - **(2)** *Interim report* Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment of this section, the Director of the National Institute of Justice shall submit to Congress a report that contains information on the progress of the Director of the National Institute of Justice in collecting the information required for the report under paragraph (1).
  > - **(c)** *Grants* In carrying out this section, the Director of the National Institute of Justice may—
  >   - **(1)** provide grants for quantitative and qualitative research designed to prevent and reduce deaths in custody; and
  >   - **(2)** contract with or provide grants to any entity the Director of the National Institute of Justice determines appropriate to facilitate the identification and reporting of the information required under this section.

  > ## § 6. Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics requirements relating to death in custody
  >
  > - **(a)** *Annual report* Not later than July 31 of each year, the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics shall submit to the Committees on the Judiciary of the House of Representatives and the Senate, and make available on a publicly accessible website of the Bureau of Justice Statistics, a report that contains the following:
  >   - **(1)** The number of deaths in custody, searchable across preceding reporting years.
  >   - **(2)** The number of deaths in custody, disaggregated by—
  >     - **(A)** age, gender, race, and ethnicity;
  >     - **(B)** the facility where the deceased was in custody;
  >     - **(C)** the location where the death occurred;
  >     - **(D)** the cause of death; and
  >     - **(E)** the manner of death.
  >   - **(3)** The number of deaths in custody that occurred not later than 30 days after release from custody if, at the time of release—
  >     - **(A)** the deceased was in a healthcare facility or was being transported to a healthcare facility;
  >     - **(B)** the release was the result of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased, including compassionate release, medical parole, or medical furlough; or
  >     - **(C)** the release was advanced by or coincided with the onset, diagnosis, or material worsening of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased.
  >   - **(4)** Information on the cause of death, the manner of death, and any contributing conditions, as contained on the United States standard certificate of death.
  >   - **(5)** A multi-year analysis comparing the information from paragraphs (1) through (4) for a period to be determined by the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics.
  >   - **(6)** Information on identified limitations on the information included in the report, including limitations on coverage, comparability, and completeness.
  >   - **(7)** A description of the sources of information used in the report, including use of reports submitted under sections 3(a) and 4(a), open-source information, and death certificate data.
  >   - **(8)** A description of statistical methods applied to produce any figures included in the report.
  >   - **(9)** The report submitted by the Panel under section 7(c)(2).
  > - **(b)** *Data publication*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* The Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics shall make available on a publicly accessible website of the Bureau of Justice Statistics a database on deaths in custody.
  >   - **(2)** *Database contents* The database under paragraph (1) shall include the following, with information appropriately de-identified:
  >     - **(A)** The information reported under sections 3(a) and 4(a).
  >     - **(B)** The cause of death and the manner of death, categorized according to the consensus definitions developed under subsection (d).
  >   - **(3)** *Form* The information required under paragraph (2) shall be made available in a form that is machine-readable and searchable, with delineated columns for each category of information required.
  > - **(c)** *Open-Source information*
  >   - **(1)** *Use of open-source information* The Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics shall develop and implement methodologies to use open-source information to supplement the information reported under sections 3(a) and 4(a).
  >   - **(2)** *Submission of open-source information* The Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics shall establish and maintain a tool to allow individuals and organizations to submit information on deaths in custody.
  > - **(d)** *Consensus definitions* The Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics, in consultation with the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics, shall develop consensus definitions to categorize the manner of each death in custody as a homicide, suicide, accident, natural death, or death of an undetermined cause.

  > ## § 7. Review panel
  >
  > - **(a)** *Establishment* Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment of this section, the Attorney General shall establish a review panel on deaths in Federal or State custody (in this Act referred to as the “Panel”) to—
  >   - **(1)** assess compliance with this Act, with emphasis on reports that are missing, late, or determined not credible;
  >   - **(2)** examine barriers to accurate and timely reporting;
  >   - **(3)** hold public hearings to present findings, solicit feedback, and receive public comment;
  >   - **(4)** review the reports under sections 5 and 6 to identify key findings and omissions; and
  >   - **(5)** submit recommendations to the Director of the National Institute of Justice, the Secretary of Health and Human Services, each State, and the Director of the Bureau of Prisons to improve the quality of information reported under sections 3(a) and 4(a).
  > - **(b)** *Membership*
  >   - **(1)** *Members*
  >     - **(A)** *Appointment* The Panel shall be comprised of 6 members, appointed by the Attorney General in consultation with the Secretary of Health and Human Services and including—
  >       - **(i)** not fewer than 1 member with expertise in vital statistics, mortality data, or public health, selected in consultation with the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics;
  >       - **(ii)** not fewer than 1 member who is a licensed physician who—
  >         - **(I)** is a board-certified forensic pathologist; and
  >         - **(II)** has served as a medical examiner or coroner;
  >       - **(iii)** not fewer than 1 member with expertise in—
  >         - **(I)** correctional facility research and operations; and
  >         - **(II)** custody-related mortality;
  >       - **(iv)** not fewer than 1 member with expertise in—
  >         - **(I)** public health; or
  >         - **(II)** biostatistics;
  >       - **(v)** not fewer than 1 member who—
  >         - **(I)** is a member of—
  >           - **(aa)** the National Academy of Sciences; or
  >           - **(bb)** the National Academy of Medicine; and
  >         - **(II)** is selected in consultation with the academy in which such individual is a member; and
  >       - **(vi)** not fewer than 1 member who—
  >         - **(I)** is a family member of an individual who died in custody; or
  >         - **(II)** is formerly incarcerated.
  >     - **(B)** *Period of appointment*
  >       - **(i)** *In general* Members of the Panel shall serve staggered 3-year terms.
  >       - **(ii)** *Staggered terms* Of the members of the Panel initially appointed under this Act, as designated at the time of appointment—
  >         - **(I)** one-third shall serve for a term of 1 year;
  >         - **(II)** one-third shall serve for a term of 2 years; and
  >         - **(III)** one-third shall serve for a term of 3 years.
  >     - **(C)** *Service restriction* A member of the Panel may not hold any office, position, or employment, in—
  >       - **(i)** a State or local law enforcement agency covered by the reporting requirements under section 3(a); or
  >       - **(ii)** a Federal law enforcement agency required to make reports under section 4(a).
  >     - **(D)** *Compensation* A member of the Panel shall serve without compensation but may be reimbursed in accordance with section 5703 of title 5, United States Code.
  >   - **(2)** *Chair*
  >     - **(A)** *Appointment* In addition to the members under paragraph (1), the Attorney General shall appoint a non-voting chair of the Panel, to serve for a term of 3 years.
  >     - **(B)** *Qualifications* The chair of the panel shall meet not less than 1 of the criteria in paragraph (1)(A).
  >     - **(C)** *Service restriction* The chair of the Panel may not hold any office, position, or employment, in—
  >       - **(i)** a State or local law enforcement agency covered by the reporting requirements under section 3(a); or
  >       - **(ii)** a Federal law enforcement agency required to make reports under section 4(a).
  >     - **(D)** *Compensation* The chair of the Panel shall serve without compensation but may be reimbursed in accordance with section 5703 of title 5, United States Code.
  > - **(c)** *Duties*
  >   - **(1)** *Annual public hearings*
  >     - **(A)** *In general* The Panel shall hold public hearings on an annual basis.
  >     - **(B)** *Hearing contents* A hearing under subparagraph (A) shall address a State or head of a Federal law enforcement agency required to make reports under section 3(a) or 4(a), respectively, that—
  >       - **(i)** has failed to report required information, has reported such information late, or has reported information determined not credible by the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics;
  >       - **(ii)** has reported information that is inconsistent with information collected by the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics, as determined in a written record made available to such State or head of a Federal law enforcement agency not less than 60 days before such hearing; or
  >       - **(iii)** has reported information that the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics identifies as requiring review to ensure completeness and reliability of information on deaths in custody.
  >     - **(C)** *Prohibition* In conducting a hearing under subparagraph (A), the Panel may not—
  >       - **(i)** rank custodial systems by the incidence of death in custody for the purposes of compliance and enforcement; or
  >       - **(ii)** facilitate a forum for penalizing a jurisdiction for a higher incidence of death in custody.
  >     - **(D)** *Testimony* The Panel shall—
  >       - **(i)** request and obtain the testimony of—
  >         - **(I)** an official responsible for making reports on behalf of a State under section 3(a); and
  >         - **(II)** the head of a Federal law enforcement agency responsible for making reports under section 4(a); and
  >       - **(ii)** receive testimony, on request or by voluntary submission, from—
  >         - **(I)** a family member of a person who died in custody;
  >         - **(II)** a non-profit organization with a significant focus on individuals who are detained by or likely to come into contact with law enforcement;
  >         - **(III)** a medical examiner or forensic pathologist;
  >         - **(IV)** a statistician;
  >         - **(V)** a researcher;
  >         - **(VI)** a journalist; and
  >         - **(VII)** any other party with information that the Panel determines relevant.
  >     - **(E)** *Subpoena authority*
  >       - **(i)** *In general* The Panel may issue a subpoena to require the attendance and testimony of witnesses and the production of evidence relevant to determining compliance with this Act.
  >       - **(ii)** *Compliance with subpoena* If an individual refuses to obey a subpoena issued under clause (i), the Attorney General may bring an action in the United States district court with jurisdiction over such person for compliance with such subpoena.
  >   - **(2)** *Report to Attorney General* On an annual basis, the Panel shall submit to the Attorney General a report that includes—
  >     - **(A)** recommendations related to—
  >       - **(i)** best practices to improve the accuracy and timeliness of reporting under sections 3(a) and 4(a);
  >       - **(ii)** strategies to reduce deaths in custody, informed by evidence presented at the annual hearing required under paragraph (1); and
  >       - **(iii)** necessary improvements to the consensus definitions developed under section 6(d) and the data collection protocols developed under section 6(c);
  >     - **(B)** identification of each State or Federal law enforcement agency required to report information under section 3(a) or 4(a), respectively, that—
  >       - **(i)** failed to report information, as determined by the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics; or
  >       - **(ii)** submitted incomplete information that undercounts the number of deaths in custody, as determined through quantitative and qualitative methods; and
  >     - **(C)** a summary of—
  >       - **(i)** the nature of the failure of each State or Federal law enforcement agency identified under subparagraph (B); and
  >       - **(ii)** the efforts of the Attorney General to obtain compliance.
  > - **(d)** *Exemption from section 1013 of title 5* Section 1013 of title 5, United States Code, shall not apply to the Board.

  > ## § 8. National Center for Health Statistics
  >
  > - **(a)** *Identification of deaths in custody*
  >   - **(1)** *United States standard certificate of death* Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment of this section, the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics shall include on the United States standard certificate of death a checkbox, or other functionally equivalent designation, to indicate whether the death of the deceased was a death in custody.
  >   - **(2)** *State certificates of death* The Director of the National Center for Health Statistics shall provide to State vital registration officials guidance, model language, and technical assistance to facilitate the adoption of a checkbox, or other functionally equivalent designation, to indicate whether the death of the deceased was a death in custody, on the certificate of death of each State.
  > - **(b)** *National Death Index*
  >   - **(1)** *Publicly available information* Notwithstanding section 308(d) of the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 242m(d)) or sections 3561 through 3583 of title 44, United States Code, the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics shall make available to qualified requesters, at no cost, all records, services, and matches of the National Death Index, including information related to cause of death—
  >     - **(A)** with respect to any death identified as a death in custody on a certificate of death; or
  >     - **(B)** for which a requestor certifies in writing, at such time, in such manner, and containing such information as the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics may require, a request is made for the purpose of researching, reporting on, or documenting deaths in custody.
  >   - **(2)** *Cost recovery* Sums authorized under sections 8 and 12 shall be available to the Secretary of Health and Human Services to reimburse the costs of services provided under paragraph (1).
  > - **(c)** *Cooperative agreements* To carry out this section, the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics may enter into cooperative agreements with—
  >   - **(1)** State and local vital registration officials;
  >   - **(2)** the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics; and
  >   - **(3)** any other Federal, State, or local entity the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics determines appropriate.

  > ## § 9. Immigration detention
  >
  > - **(a)** *Death disclosure* Not later than 48 hours after a covered death occurs, the Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection shall submit to the appropriate committees of Congress, and thereafter publish on the website of the Department of Homeland Security, a notice of such covered death.
  > - **(b)** *Death review*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* Not later than 90 days after a covered death occurs, the Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection shall complete an investigation into each covered death to determine the manner of death and the cause of death.
  >   - **(2)** *Investigation* An investigation under paragraph (1) shall—
  >     - **(A)** examine the facts and circumstances surrounding the detention and death of the deceased, including a review of—
  >       - **(i)** the forensic pathology report of the deceased, including the autopsy if available;
  >       - **(ii)** any eyewitness or video accounts related to the death;
  >       - **(iii)** any physical evidence obtained from the scene of the death; and
  >       - **(iv)** any relevant medical records of the deceased; and
  >     - **(B)** determine whether the deceased received medical treatment in accordance with community and professional standards of care.
  > - **(c)** *Report on deaths in custody*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* Not later than 90 days after a covered death occurs, the Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement or the Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection shall submit to appropriate committees of Congress, and thereafter make available on a publicly accessible website of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and on a publicly accessible website of U.S. Customs and Border Protection, a report on each covered death.
  >   - **(2)** *Report contents* A report under paragraph (1) shall include, with respect to a covered death, the following:
  >     - **(A)** Information on the deceased, including—
  >       - **(i)** age, gender, race, and ethnicity;
  >       - **(ii)** the facility where the deceased was in custody;
  >       - **(iii)** the location where the death occurred;
  >       - **(iv)** the cause of death; and
  >       - **(v)** the manner of death.
  >     - **(B)** Whether the death occurred not later than 30 days after release from custody if, at the time of release—
  >       - **(i)** the deceased was in a healthcare facility, was being transported to a healthcare facility, or was scheduled for imminent medical or psychiatric treatment for an acute condition;
  >       - **(ii)** the release was the result of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased, including compassionate release, medical parole, or medical furlough; or
  >       - **(iii)** the release was advanced by or coincided with the onset, diagnosis, or material worsening of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased.
  >     - **(C)** Information on the cause of death, the manner of death, and any contributing conditions, as contained on the United States standard certificate of death.
  >     - **(D)** A description of the sources of information used in the report.
  > - **(d)** *Notification to Congress* The Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Director of Customs and Border Protection shall submit to Congress a joint annual report that—
  >   - **(1)** includes categories based on the cause and manner of death;
  >   - **(2)** assesses whether there is a pattern of covered deaths with respect to the facility and across facilities in which deaths occurred; and
  >   - **(3)** makes recommendations for how covered deaths can be prevented; and
  >   - **(4)** includes limitations on the information included in the death reports, including limitations on coverage, comparability, and completeness.
  > - **(e)** *Covered death* In this section—
  >   - **(1)** the term “appropriate committees of Congress” means—
  >     - **(A)** the Committees on the Judiciary, Homeland Security, Oversight and Government Reform, and Appropriations of the House of Representatives; and
  >     - **(B)** the Committees on the Judiciary, Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs, and Appropriations of the Senate; and
  >   - **(2)** the term “covered death” means a death that occurred—
  >     - **(A)** when the deceased was detained, under arrest, being pursued by, or being arrested by a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer or by a U.S. Customs and Border Protection officer (or by any Federal, State, or local law enforcement officer for purposes of assisting U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement or U.S. Customs and Border Protection); or
  >     - **(B)** not later than 30 days after release from custody of an immigration detention facility.

  > ## § 10. Privacy exemption
  >
  > Notwithstanding regulations promulgated under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, at part 160 of title 45, Code of Federal Regulations, and subparts A and E of part 164 of such title (or any successor regulations), and sections 304 and 812 of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968 (34 U.S.C. 10134 and 10231), and any rule or regulation promulgated thereunder, the information reported under this Act shall be considered public information.

  > ## § 11. Definitions
  >
  > In this Act:
  >
  > - **(1)** *Boot camp prison* The term “boot camp prison” has the meaning given such term in section 901 of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968 (34 U.S.C. 10251).
  > - **(2)** *Death in custody* The term “death in custody” means death in Federal custody and death in State custody.
  > - **(3)** *Death in Federal custody* The term “death in Federal custody” means a death that occurred when the deceased was in the custody of the Federal Government, or under color of or pursuant to Federal law, or that otherwise involved an employee or agent of the Federal Government, including a death that occurred—
  >   - **(A)** when the deceased was—
  >     - **(i)** detained, under arrest, being pursued by, fleeing from, or in the process of being arrested by any officer of a Federal law enforcement agency (or by any State or local law enforcement officer while participating in and for purposes of a Federal law enforcement operation, task force, or any other Federal law enforcement capacity carried out by such Federal law enforcement agency); or
  >     - **(ii)** en route to being incarcerated or detained, or incarcerated or detained, at, or being removed from—
  >       - **(I)** any facility (including an immigration detention facility or juvenile facility) pursuant to a contract with a Federal law enforcement agency or acting in such capacity;
  >       - **(II)** any State or local government facility used by a Federal law enforcement agency; or
  >       - **(III)** any Federal correctional facility or Federal pretrial detention facility located within the United States;
  >   - **(B)** not later than 30 days after release from custody if at the time of release—
  >     - **(i)** the deceased was in a healthcare facility or was being transported to a healthcare facility;
  >     - **(ii)** the release was the result of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased, including compassionate release, medical parole, or medical furlough; or
  >     - **(iii)** the release was advanced by or coincided with the onset, diagnosis, or material worsening of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased;
  >   - **(C)** not later than 30 days after release from custody if the deceased was released from an immigration detention facility.
  > - **(4)** *Death in State custody* The term “death in State custody” means a death that occurred when the deceased was in the custody of the State, or that otherwise involved an employee or agent of the State, including a death that occurred—
  >   - **(A)** when the deceased was—
  >     - **(i)** detained, under arrest, being pursued by, fleeing from, or in the process of being arrested by State or local law enforcement;
  >     - **(ii)** en route to being incarcerated;
  >     - **(iii)** incarcerated at—
  >       - **(I)** a municipal or county jail;
  >       - **(II)** a police lock-up;
  >       - **(III)** State prison;
  >       - **(IV)** State-run boot camp prison;
  >       - **(V)** boot camp prison that is contracted out by the State;
  >       - **(VI)** any facility used by a State or unit of local government pursuant to a contract or other agreement, such as a memorandum of understanding; or
  >       - **(VII)** other local or State correctional or detention facility or area (including any juvenile facility); or
  >     - **(iv)** was being removed from any facility described in clause (iii);
  >   - **(B)** not later than 30 days after, release from custody if at the time of release—
  >     - **(i)** the deceased was in a healthcare facility or was being transported to a healthcare facility;
  >     - **(ii)** the release was the result of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased, including compassionate release, medical parole, or medical furlough; or
  >     - **(iii)** the release was advanced by or coincided with the onset, diagnosis, or material worsening of the medical or psychiatric condition of the deceased.
  > - **(5)** *Director of the Bureau of Justice Assistance* The term “Director of the Bureau of Justice Assistance” means the Attorney General, acting through the Director of the Bureau of Justice Assistance of the Department of Justice.
  > - **(6)** *Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics* The term “Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics” means the Attorney General, acting through the Director of the Bureau of Justice Statistics of the Department of Justice.
  > - **(7)** *Director of the National Center for Health Statistics* The term “Director of the National Center for Health Statistics” means the Secretary of Health and Human Services, acting through the Director of the National Center for Health Statistics of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
  > - **(8)** *Director of the National Institute of Justice* The term “Director of the National Institute of Justice” means the Attorney General, acting through the Director of the National Institute of Justice of the Department of Justice.
  > - **(9)** *Immigration detention facility* The term “immigration detention facility”—
  >   - **(A)** means a secure facility that detains individuals who are charged, or awaiting such charges, with violations of immigration law or individuals who are awaiting deportation; and
  >   - **(B)** includes—
  >     - **(i)** such facilities that are either owned or operated by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement;
  >     - **(ii)** such facilities that are either owned or operated by U.S. Customs and Border Protection; and
  >     - **(iii)** such facilities, whether public or private, that are operated through a contract with the Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement or the Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Patrol.
  > - **(10)** *Open-source information* The term “open-source information” means information that is publicly accessible and includes information posted online or made publicly accessible under section 552 of title 5, United States Code.
  > - **(11)** *Police lock-up* The term “police lock-up” means a temporary location for detention that is used for the confinement of persons under arrest before such persons are brought before a court.
  > - **(12)** *State* The term “State” has the meaning given such term in section 901 of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968 (34 U.S.C. 10251).
