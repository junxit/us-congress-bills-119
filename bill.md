# H. RES. 1657

> Condemning the Taliban’s systematic erasure of women and girls from public life in Afghanistan, and calling for United States leadership in response.

## Preamble

- Whereas since seizing power in August 2021, the Taliban have issued edicts that bar Afghan girls from secondary school and women from universities;
- Whereas the Taliban have barred women from most forms of employment, restricted their freedom of movement without a male guardian, and closed spaces where women gather;
- Whereas in August 2024, the Taliban formalized rules that prohibit women's voices and faces in public, extending a pattern of erasing women from civic life;
- Whereas Afghanistan is the only country in the world that bans girls and women from secondary and higher education;
- Whereas the International Criminal Court has sought and issued arrest warrants for senior Taliban leaders for the crime against humanity of gender persecution;
- Whereas the erasure of Afghan women and girls harms Afghan families, the economy, and public health, and undermines regional stability and United States national security interests;
- Whereas Afghan women have continued to teach, learn, and organize in secret at great personal risk; and
- Whereas the protection of women's and girls' rights has long drawn support from Members of both parties: Now, therefore, be it

That the House of Representatives—

- **(1)** condemns in the strongest terms the Taliban's systematic and institutionalized repression of Afghan women and girls;
- **(2)** affirms that no government that denies half its population education, work, and a public voice can claim legitimacy;
- **(3)** honors the courage of Afghan women and girls who continue to learn, teach, and speak out;
- **(4)** calls on the President to make the restoration of women's and girls' education and freedom of movement a standing priority in all diplomatic engagement concerning Afghanistan;
- **(5)** urges the use of targeted sanctions and other tools against Taliban officials responsible for these abuses;
- **(6)** supports humanitarian aid and underground and remote education programs that reach Afghan women and girls without benefiting the Taliban; and
- **(7)** urges allies and international bodies to coordinate pressure and to support accountability for gender persecution.
