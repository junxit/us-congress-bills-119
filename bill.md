# S. RES. 962

> Designating the week of November 27 through December 3, 2026, as “National Electronic Transactions Week”.

## Preamble

- Whereas electronic transactions are essential for commerce in the United States, accounting for approximately 70 percent of consumer spending in the United States;
- Whereas people in the United States made 236,600,000,000 noncash transactions in 2024, with a total value of $140,010,000,000;
- Whereas the electronic transactions industry contributed more than $354,000,000,000 to the gross domestic product of the United States and supported approximately 2,000,000 jobs in all 50 States in 2024;
- Whereas the holiday shopping season represents one of the most significant periods of economic activity in the United States;
- Whereas the 7 days after Thanksgiving encompass Black Friday, Small Business Saturday, Cyber Monday, and GivingTuesday, creating a unique period in which people in the United States use electronic transactions to shop, support local businesses, conduct commerce online, give to charitable organizations, and participate in their communities;
- Whereas Cyber Week, the 5-day period from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday, generated a record $44,200,000,000 in online sales in 2025;
- Whereas the 2025 holiday season generated a record $257,800,000,000 in online sales from November 1 through December 31, an increase of 6.8 percent from the previous year, with mobile devices accounting for 56.4 percent of online transactions;
- Whereas 202,900,000 people in the United States shopped during the 5-day Thanksgiving holiday weekend in 2025;
- Whereas Small Business Saturday provides consumers with an opportunity to support locally-owned, independently-operated, and family-owned businesses, while electronic transactions enable such businesses to accept secure and convenient payments from customers in their communities and beyond;
- Whereas payment innovations enable businesses, particularly small businesses, to make an estimated $34,000,000,000 in additional annual sales, supporting an estimated 301,000 jobs and $28,000,000,000 of the gross domestic product of the United States;
- Whereas the 7 days after Thanksgiving also include GivingTuesday, providing people in the United States with an opportunity to support charitable organizations and causes in communities across the country;
- Whereas GivingTuesday 2025 generated $4,000,000,000 in donations in the United States;
- Whereas 38,100,000 people in the United States participated in GivingTuesday 2025, including 19,100,000 people who made financial contributions;
- Whereas electronic transactions provide consumers with secure, convenient, and efficient ways to make charitable contributions, helping connect donors with nonprofit organizations and causes serving communities across the United States;
- Whereas the 7 days after Thanksgiving therefore provide a unique opportunity to recognize the many ways electronic transactions connect people in the United States to commerce;
- Whereas consumers and merchants depend on the reliability, security, speed, and convenience of electronic payment systems during the holiday season and throughout the year;
- Whereas the holiday season represents an important period for consumers and merchants in the United States both economically and culturally; and
- Whereas electronic transactions play an integral role for consumers and merchants in the transfer of goods and services not only during the holiday season, but throughout the year: Now, therefore, be it

That the Senate—

- **(1)** designates the week of November 27 through December 3, 2026, as “National Electronic Transactions Week”;
- **(2)** recognizes the important role of electronic transactions in Black Friday, Small Business Saturday, Cyber Monday, and GivingTuesday;
- **(3)** recognizes the significant contributions and importance of electronic transactions to consumers, merchants, small businesses, charitable organizations, and the economy of the United States;
- **(4)** recognizes the role electronic transactions play in supporting holiday commerce, small businesses and entrepreneurs, charitable giving, and community participation throughout the United States;
- **(5)** recognizes the continued investment by transactions and technology companies in the United States in innovation, security, fraud and scam prevention, infrastructure, and resilience; and
- **(6)** encourages consumers, businesses, policymakers, and other stakeholders to recognize National Electronic Transactions Week and the important role electronic transactions play in connecting the people of the United States to commerce, opportunity, and community throughout the United States.
