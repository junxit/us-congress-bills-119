# H.R. 11125

> To authorize the Secretary of the Interior to cooperate with the State of California and other appropriate entities in conducting a hydrogeologic characterization, mapping, and modeling program for priority transboundary aquifers, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Transboundary Groundwater Research and Mapping Act”.

## § 2. Definitions

In this Act:

- **(1)** *Aquifer* The term aquifer has the meaning given the term in section 3 of the United States-Mexico Transboundary Aquifer Assessment Act (42 U.S.C. 1962 note).
- **(2)** *Covered area* The term covered area means—
  - **(A)** the area depicted as the “Valle de Mexicali-San Luis Rio Colorado Aquifer/Yuma-Imperial Valley” transboundary aquifer on the map titled “Transboundary Aquifers of the World,” published by the International Groundwater Resources Assessment Centre (IGRAC), 2025 edition; and
  - **(B)** the area depicted as “Study area” on the map titled “San Diego Hydrogeology Project Study Area” and dated 2012.
- **(3)** *IBWC* The term IBWC has the meaning given the term in section 3 of the United States-Mexico Transboundary Aquifer Assessment Act (42 U.S.C. 1962 note).
- **(4)** *Indian tribe* The term Indian tribe has the meaning given the term in section 3 of the United States-Mexico Transboundary Aquifer Assessment Act (42 U.S.C. 1962 note).
- **(5)** *Priority transboundary aquifer* The term priority transboundary aquifer means a transboundary aquifer that has been designated for study and analysis under section 3(c).
- **(6)** *Program* The term Program means the United States-Mexico transboundary aquifer assessment program established under section 3(a).
- **(7)** *Secretary* The term Secretary means the Secretary of the Interior, acting through the Director of the United States Geological Survey.
- **(8)** *Transboundary aquifer* The term transboundary aquifer means an aquifer located entirely or partially within the covered area that underlies the boundary between the State of California and Mexico.
- **(9)** *Water resources research institute* The term water resources research institute means the institute within California established under section 104 of the Water Resources Research Act of 1984 (42 U.S.C. 10303).

## § 3. United States-Mexico transboundary aquifer assessment program

- **(a)** *In general* The Secretary, in consultation and cooperation with the State of California, the water resources research institute, and other appropriate entities in the United States and Mexico, and the IBWC, as appropriate, shall carry out a United States-Mexico transboundary aquifer assessment program to characterize, map, and model priority transboundary aquifers at a level of detail determined to be appropriate by the Secretary for the particular aquifer.
- **(b)** *Objectives* The objectives of the Program are to—
  - **(1)** develop and implement an integrated scientific approach to identify and assess priority transboundary aquifers, including—
    - **(A)** specifying priority transboundary aquifers for further analysis by assessing—
      - **(i)** the proximity of a proposed priority transboundary aquifer to areas of high population density or significant agricultural production;
      - **(ii)** the extent to which a proposed priority transboundary aquifer is used or could be used;
      - **(iii)** the susceptibility of a proposed priority transboundary aquifer to contamination;
      - **(iv)** the rate of overdraft within a proposed priority transboundary aquifer; and
      - **(v)** any other relevant criteria;
    - **(B)** evaluating all relevant available data and publications as part of the development of a study plan under section 4(c) for each priority transboundary aquifer;
    - **(C)** creating a new, or enhancing an existing, geographic information system database to characterize the spatial and temporal aspects of each priority transboundary aquifer; and
    - **(D)** using remote sensing or field studies, including support for and expansion of ongoing monitoring and metering efforts, to develop—
      - **(i)** any additional data necessary to adequately define aquifer characteristics; and
      - **(ii)** scientifically sound groundwater flow models to assist with State and local water management and administration, including modeling of relevant groundwater and surface water interactions;
  - **(2)** consider the expansion or modification of existing agreements between the United States Geological Survey, the State of California, the water resources research institute, and appropriate authorities in the United States and Mexico, to—
    - **(A)** conduct joint scientific investigations;
    - **(B)** develop joint scientific aquifer monitoring programs for groundwater level, land subsidence, water quality;
    - **(C)** archive and share relevant data; and
    - **(D)** carry out any other activities consistent with the Program; and
  - **(3)** produce scientific products for each priority transboundary aquifer that—
    - **(A)** are capable of being broadly distributed; and
    - **(B)** provide the scientific information needed by water managers and natural resource agencies on both sides of the United States-Mexico border to effectively accomplish the missions of the managers and agencies.
- **(c)** *Designation of priority transboundary aquifers*
  - **(1)** *In general* For purposes of the Program, the Secretary shall designate as priority transboundary aquifers the transboundary aquifers underlying—
    - **(A)** the Coastal Plain of San Diego Groundwater Basin underlying southwestern San Diego County;
    - **(B)** the Imperial Valley Groundwater Basin underlying Imperial Valley, California;
    - **(C)** the Ogilby Valley Groundwater Basin underlying Ogilby Valley, California;
    - **(D)** the Coyote Wells Valley Groundwater Basin underlying Coyote Wells Valley, California; and
    - **(E)** the Yuma Valley Groundwater Basin underlying the Yuma Valley, California.
  - **(2)** *Additional aquifers* The Secretary may, using the criteria under subsection (b)(1)(A), evaluate and designate additional priority transboundary aquifers that underlie the covered area.
- **(d)** *Cooperation with Mexico* To ensure a comprehensive assessment of priority transboundary aquifers, the Secretary shall, to the maximum extent practicable, work with appropriate Federal agencies and other organizations to develop partnerships with, and receive input from, relevant organizations in Mexico to carry out the Program.
- **(e)** *Grants and cooperative agreements* The Secretary may provide grants or enter into agreements, including cooperative agreements, with the water resources research institute and other entities located within California to carry out the Program.

## § 4. Implementation of program

- **(a)** *Coordination with California, Indian Tribes, and other entities* The Secretary shall coordinate the activities carried out under the Program with—
  - **(1)** the California Department of Water Resources;
  - **(2)** the California State Water Resources Control Board;
  - **(3)** any affected Indian Tribes;
  - **(4)** any other appropriate entities that are conducting monitoring and metering activity with respect to a priority transboundary aquifer; and
  - **(5)** the IBWC, as appropriate.
- **(b)** *New activity* After the date of the enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall not initiate any new field studies or analyses with respect to a transboundary aquifer under the Program before consulting with, and coordinating the activity with, the appropriate water resource agency of California.
- **(c)** *Study plans; cost estimates*
  - **(1)** *In general* The Secretary shall work closely with the appropriate California water resource agency, the water resources research institute, and other relevant entities to develop a study plan, timeline, and cost estimate for each priority transboundary aquifer to be studied under the Program.
  - **(2)** *Requirements* A study plan developed under paragraph (1) shall, to the maximum extent practicable—
    - **(A)** integrate existing data collection and analyses conducted with respect to the priority transboundary aquifer;
    - **(B)** if applicable, improve and strengthen existing groundwater flow models developed for the priority transboundary aquifer; and
    - **(C)** be consistent with appropriate State guidelines and goals.

## § 5. Effect

- **(a)** *In general* Nothing in this Act affects—
  - **(1)** the jurisdiction or responsibility of California with respect to managing surface or groundwater resources in California;
  - **(2)** the water rights of any person or entity using water from a transboundary aquifer; or
  - **(3)** State water law, or an interstate compact or international treaty governing water.
- **(b)** *Treaty* Nothing in this Act shall delay or alter the implementation or operation of any works constructed, modified, acquired, or used within the territorial limits of the United States relating to the waters governed by the Treaty Between the United States and Mexico Regarding Utilization of Waters of the Colorado and Tijuana Rivers and of the Rio Grande, Treaty Series 994 (59 Stat. 1219).

## § 6. Reports

Not later than 5 years after the date of the enactment of this Act, and on completion of the Program in fiscal year 2037, the Secretary shall submit to the appropriate water resource agency in California an interim and final report, respectively, that describes—

- **(1)** any activities carried out under the Program;
- **(2)** any conclusions of the Secretary relating to the status of priority transboundary aquifers; and
- **(3)** the level of participation in the program of entities in Mexico.

## § 7. Authorization of appropriations

- **(a)** *In general* There are authorized to be appropriated to the Secretary to carry out this Act $25,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 2037.
- **(b)** *Distribution of funds* Of the amounts made available pursuant to subsection (a), 50 percent shall be made available to the water resources research institute—
  - **(1)** to provide funding to appropriate entities in California, including State agencies, universities, and other relevant organizations; and
  - **(2)** to implement cooperative agreements entered into with appropriate entities in Mexico to conduct specific authorized activities in furtherance of the Program, including the binational collection and exchange of scientific data.
- **(c)** *Criteria* Funding provided by the water resources research institute to an appropriate entity in Mexico pursuant to subsection (b) shall be contingent on that entity providing 50 percent of the necessary resources, including in-kind services, to further assist in carrying out the authorized activity.

## § 8. Sunset of authority

The authority of the Secretary to carry out any provisions of this Act shall terminate on the date that is 10 years after the date of the enactment of this Act.
