# H.R. 10981

> To direct the Secretary of Commerce to promulgate regulations related to certain artificial intelligence models, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “American AI Security Act”.

## § 2. Regulations relating to covered foundation models

- **(a)** *In general* Not later than 6 months after the date of the enactment of this Act, the Secretary, in coordination with the Secretary of Defense, acting through the Director of the National Security Agency, and the actors described in subsection (d), shall promulgate such regulations as necessary to carry out the following:
  - **(1)** Identify which foundation model shall be considered a covered foundation model under this section, including by identifying the following:
    - **(A)** A minimum parameter threshold for a covered foundation model.
    - **(B)** Any capability demonstrated by a covered foundation model that could do any of the following:
      - **(i)** Pose a serious risk to national security, including by conducting a cybersecurity threat.
      - **(ii)** Materially increase, as determined by the Secretary, the ability of an individual, organization, or foreign nation to develop a chemical weapon, biological weapon, or radiological weapon.
  - **(2)** Require a developer of a covered foundation model to provide to the Director of the National Security Agency, prior to the initial deployment of a covered foundation model and prior to the deployment of a covered foundation model that has undergone a substantial modification, full access to the covered foundation model and information related to the following for an evaluation under subparagraph (A) of subsection (c)(1):
    - **(A)** How the developer trained the covered foundation model.
    - **(B)** Any safety tests performed by the developer with respect to such covered foundation model and the results of any such safety tests.
    - **(C)** How the developer intends to deploy the covered foundation model.
    - **(D)** Any individual, organization, or entity the developer of the covered foundation model intends to grant access to the covered foundation model.
  - **(3)** Prohibit a developer of a covered foundation model from deploying the covered foundation model unless any of the following conditions is satisfied:
    - **(A)** The Director of the National Security Agency, in consultation with the Secretary and the actors described in subsection (d), has conducted an evaluation under subparagraph (A) of subsection (c)(1), and developed recommendations under subparagraph (B) of such subsection, and the developer has implemented such recommendations.
    - **(B)** The Director of the National Security Agency, in consultation with the Secretary and the actors described in subsection (d), has not carried out the requirements under subsection (c) during the period of time specified in such subsection.
  - **(4)** Identify what modification shall be considered a substantial modification with respect to a covered foundation model.
- **(b)** *Updates* Not later than 2 years after the date of the enactment of this Act and annually thereafter, the Secretary, in coordination with the Secretary of Defense, acting through the Director of the National Security Agency, and the actors described in subsection (d), shall review any regulation promulgated pursuant to subsection (a) and update any such regulation as the Secretary determines necessary.
- **(c)** *Evaluation*
  - **(1)** *In general* Not later than 30 days after the date on which a developer of a covered foundation model provides to the Director of the National Security Agency access to and information relating to the covered foundation model pursuant to a regulation promulgated under subsection (a)(2), the Director of the National Security Agency, in consultation with the Secretary and the actors described in subsection (d), shall carry out the following:
    - **(A)** Conduct an evaluation of the following:
      - **(i)** Any capability demonstrated by the covered foundation model that may create a serious cybersecurity threat if the developer made the covered foundation model publicly available, including the capability to carry out any of the following:
        - **(I)** Conduct, in whole or in part, a cybersecurity threat.
        - **(II)** Develop or exploit a vulnerability in an information system.
        - **(III)** Assist an individual, organization, or entity in evading, degrading, or bypassing a cybersecurity defense.
      - **(ii)** Any capability demonstrated by the covered foundation model related to a chemical weapon, biological weapon, or radiological weapon, including the capability to carry out any of the following:
        - **(I)** Provide information related to the development of such a weapon.
        - **(II)** Assist an individual, organization, or entity with respect to planning or executing the release of such a weapon.
    - **(B)** Develop recommendations on how to prevent any capability evaluated under clauses (i) and (ii) of subparagraph (A).
  - **(2)** *Evaluation report*
    - **(A)** *In general* Not later than 30 days after carrying out paragraph (1), the Director of the National Security Agency shall provide to the developer of the relevant covered foundation model an unclassified report that includes the following:
      - **(i)** A summary of any capability evaluated under clauses (i) and (ii) of subparagraph (A) of such paragraph.
      - **(ii)** Any recommendations developed under subparagraph (B) of such paragraph.
    - **(B)** *Classified annex* In carrying out subparagraph (A), the Director may include in a report required under such subparagraph a classified annex.
  - **(3)** *Extension of evaluation* In carrying out paragraph (1), the Director of the National Security Agency may extend an evaluation conducted under such paragraph by 30 days if the Director of the National Security Agency notifies the developer of the covered foundation model of such extension in writing not later than 30 days after the date on which the developer provides to the Director of the National Security Agency access to and information relating to the covered foundation model pursuant to a regulation promulgated under subsection (a)(2).
  - **(4)** *Exemption from FOIA* Section 552 of title 5, United States Code, does not apply with respect to an evaluation carried out under paragraph (1).
  - **(5)** *Consultation* In carrying out paragraph (1), the Director of the National Security Agency, in consultation with the Secretary and the actors described in subsection (d), may consult with developers of covered foundation models as appropriate.
- **(d)** *Actors described* The actors described in this subsection are the following:
  - **(1)** The Assistant Secretary of Defense for Nuclear Deterrence, Chemical, and Biological Defense Policy and Programs.
  - **(2)** The Director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
  - **(3)** The Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response of the Department of Health and Human Services.
  - **(4)** The Director of CAISI.
  - **(5)** The Under Secretary of Energy for Nuclear Security.
- **(e)** *Civil penalties* The Secretary may issue civil penalties in the amount of $500,000 per day of non-compliance with respect to the following:
  - **(1)** Providing access to and information relating to the covered foundation model pursuant to a regulation promulgated under subsection (a)(2).
  - **(2)** Violating a regulation promulgated under subsection (a)(3).
- **(f)** *Establishment of specialized court*
  - **(1)** *In general* There is established under Article I of the Constitution of the United States a court of review to be known as the “Artificial Intelligence Security Review Court” (in this section referred to as the “Court”).
  - **(2)** *Jurisdiction*
    - **(A)** *In general* The Court shall have jurisdiction to review an appeal filed by a developer of a covered foundation model related to any recommendations provided by the Director of the National Security Agency to the developer pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii).
    - **(B)** *Review*
      - **(i)** *Scope of review* With respect to an appeal filed by a developer of a covered foundation model related to any recommendations provided by the Director of the National Security Agency to the developer pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii), the Court may review the following:
        - **(I)** Whether the Director developed such recommendations and provided to the developer such recommendations in accordance with subsection (c).
        - **(II)** Whether the Director limited the recommendations so developed to relate to any capability evaluated under clauses (i) and (ii) of subsection (c)(A).
      - **(ii)** *Limitation* The Court may not review the means by which the Director of the National Security Agency, in consultation with the Secretary and the actors described in subsection (d), developed any recommendations provided by the Director to a developer of a covered foundation model pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii), including with respect to any classified evaluation methods, tools, or techniques of the Director.
    - **(C)** *Decision; certiorari to the Supreme Court* If the Court issues a decision that any recommendations provided by the Director of the National Security Agency to a developer of a covered foundation model pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii) were not developed and provided to the developer by the Director in accordance with subsection (c), or are not limited to relate to any capability evaluated under clauses (i) and (ii) of subsection (c)(A), the Court shall provide for the record a written statement of such decision, and, on petition of the United States for a writ of certiorari, the record shall be transmitted under seal to the Supreme Court, which shall have jurisdiction to review such decision.
  - **(3)** *Designation of judges*
    - **(A)** *In general* The Chief Justice of the United States shall publicly designate 5 district court judges to the Court—
      - **(i)** from at least 3 different United States judicial circuits;
      - **(ii)** at least 2 of whom shall reside within 20 miles of the District of Columbia; and
      - **(iii)** one of whom shall be publicly designated as the presiding judge.
    - **(B)** *Terms*
      - **(i)** *In general* Each judge designated under subparagraph (A) shall serve for a maximum of seven years and may not be eligible for redesignation, except that the judges first designated under such subparagraph shall be so designated for terms of three, five, and seven years.
      - **(ii)** *Vacancies* A vacancy in the Court shall be filled in the manner in which the original designation was made.
  - **(4)** *Timing*
    - **(A)** *In general* Not later than 30 days after the date on which the Director of the National Security Agency provides to a developer of a covered foundation model recommendations pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii)—
      - **(i)** the developer may file in the Court a notice of appeal related to any such recommendations; and
      - **(ii)** if the developer so files such a notice, provide to the Director a copy of such notice.
    - **(B)** *Opening brief* Not later than 30 days after the date on which a developer of a covered foundation model files a notice of appeal pursuant to subparagraph (A), the developer shall file an opening brief in the Court.
    - **(C)** *Response brief* Not later than 30 days after the date on which a developer of a covered foundation model files an opening brief pursuant to subparagraph (B), the Attorney General of the United States, in consultation with the Director of the National Security Agency, shall file a response brief in the Court.
    - **(D)** *Reply brief* Not later than 14 days after the date on which the Attorney General of the United States files a response brief pursuant to subparagraph (C), the relevant developer of a covered foundation model may file a reply brief in the Court.
    - **(E)** *Decision* The Court shall issue a decision related to an appeal filed by a developer of a covered foundation model pursuant to subparagraph (A) not later than 30 days after the later of the following:
      - **(i)** The date on which the last brief related to such appeal was submitted.
      - **(ii)** The date on which the last oral argument related to such appeal was presented to the Court, as applicable.
  - **(5)** *Rules and procedures*
    - **(A)** *In general* The presiding judge designated under subparagraph (A) of paragraph (3), in consultation with the other judges designated under such subparagraph, may establish such rules and procedures, and take such actions, as are reasonably necessary to carry out this subsection.
    - **(B)** *Record* Any rules and procedures established under subparagraph (A) shall be recorded and shall be transmitted to the following:
      - **(i)** All of the judges designated pursuant to paragraph (3)(A).
      - **(ii)** The Chief Justice of the United States.
  - **(6)** *Designation of counsel*
    - **(A)** *In general* To assist the Court in the consideration of any appeal filed by a developer of a covered foundation model related to any recommendations provided by the Director of the National Security Agency to the developer pursuant to subsection (c)(2)(A)(ii), the presiding judge designated under subparagraph (A) of paragraph (3) shall designate, at the request of a such developer that is concerned about the divulgence of classified material, or otherwise at the discretion of the presiding judge, one or more attorneys to review such recommendations and provide a written analysis to the presiding judge with respect to the matters specified in subclauses (I) and (II) of subsection (c)(2)(B)(i).
    - **(B)** *Security clearance* In carrying out subparagraph (A), the presiding judge shall ensure any attorney designated under such subparagraph is determined to be eligible for access to classified information necessary to participate in matters before the Court.
- **(g)** *Report to Congress* Not later than 18 months after the date on which the Secretary, in coordination with the Secretary of Defense, acting through the Director of the National Security Agency, and the actors described in subsection (d), promulgates any regulation under subsection (a), the Secretary shall submit to Congress a report that includes information relating to the following:
  - **(1)** The regulation.
  - **(2)** The efficacy of any evaluation conducted pursuant to any such regulation.
  - **(3)** Any recommendation for relevant legislative action related to such regulation.
- **(h)** *Preemption* No State or political subdivision thereof may establish, continue in effect, or enforce any law, regulation, or requirement that imposes a transparency, disclosure, or reporting obligation on a developer of a covered foundation model with respect to the following:
  - **(1)** Safety protocols.
  - **(2)** Safety and security frameworks.
  - **(3)** Capability evaluations or risk assessments.
  - **(4)** Any regulation promulgated under this section.
- **(i)** *Definitions* In this section:
  - **(1)** *AI; artificial intelligence* The terms AI and artificial intelligence have the meaning given the term artificial intelligence in section 5002 of the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9401).
  - **(2)** *Biological agent; delivery system; toxin; vector* The terms biological agent, delivery system, toxin, and vector have the meanings given such terms in section 178 of title 18, United States Code.
  - **(3)** *Biological weapon* The term biological weapon means any biological agent, toxin, delivery system, or vector that is developed, produced, stockpiled, transferred, acquired, retained, possessed, or used for use as a weapon, as described in section 175 of title 18, United States Code.
  - **(4)** *CAISI* The term CAISI means the Center for AI Standards and Innovation of the National Institute of Standards and Technology.
  - **(5)** *Chemical weapon* The term chemical weapon has the meaning given the term in section 229F of title 18, United States Code.
  - **(6)** *Cybersecurity threat* The term cybersecurity threat has the meaning given the term in section 2200 of the Homeland Security Act of 2002 (6 U.S.C. 650).
  - **(7)** *Deploy* The term deploy means an action taken by developer of a foundation model to release, sell, or otherwise provide access to an individual, organization, or entity not employed by or contracted to work for such developer.
  - **(8)** *Foundation model* The term foundation model means a software component of an information system that—
    - **(A)** implements artificial intelligence;
    - **(B)** uses computational, statistical, or machine-learning techniques to produce outputs from a defined set of inputs;
    - **(C)** is trained on a broad data set;
    - **(D)** is designed for generality of output; and
    - **(E)** is adaptable to a wide range of distinctive tasks.
  - **(9)** *Information system* The term information system has the meaning given the term in section 3502 of title 44, United States Code.
  - **(10)** *Parameter* The term parameter means a numerical value internal to a foundation model that—
    - **(A)** is derived or adjusted through the process of training the model on data, and
    - **(B)** contributes to determining any output of the model in response to an input.
  - **(11)** *Radiological weapon* The term radiological weapon means any weapon that is designed to release radiation or radioactivity at a level dangerous to human life.
  - **(12)** *Secretary* The term Secretary means the Secretary of Commerce.
