# H.R. 10609

> To prohibit the establishment, maintenance, and sharing of vehicle fingerprinting databases by the heads of Government agencies, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Don’t Spy On Me Act of 2026”.

## § 2. Prohibition on establishing, maintaining, or sharing vehicle fingerprinting databases

- **(a)** *Prohibition* The head of a Government agency may not establish or maintain a vehicle fingerprinting database.
- **(b)** *Civil action* In the case that the head of a Government agency violates the prohibition under subsection (a), any individual or entity whose information is the subject of such violation may bring a civil action in any district court of the United States against such Government agency with respect to such violation for—
  - **(1)** actual damages for an injury resulting from the violation;
  - **(2)** $25,000 if the court finds that the Government agency targeted the individual or entity in committing such violation;
  - **(3)** injunctive relief with respect to such violation; and
  - **(4)** reasonable attorney’s fees and costs with respect to such violation.
- **(c)** *Definitions* In this section:
  - **(1)** *Government agency* The term Government agency has the meaning given that term in section 4 of the United States Information and Educational Exchange Act of 1948 (22 U.S.C. 1433).
  - **(2)** *Vehicle fingerprinting database* The term vehicle fingerprinting database means a database in which the name of an individual or entity or the license plate of a vehicle is linked to any other identifiable information obtained from an automated license plate reader, including—
    - **(A)** any characteristic of the vehicle, such as the presence of a bumper sticker or roof rack on the vehicle, damage to the vehicle, or the make, model, or color of the vehicle; or
    - **(B)** geolocation data, including any location visited and the frequency of any such visit.
