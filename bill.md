# H.R. 11106

> To prevent Federal benefits fraud, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Federal Accountability for Fraud Offenders Act”.

## § 2. Definitions

In this Act:

- **(1)** *Federal benefit program* The term “Federal benefit program” means a program established or authorized by Federal law that provides payments, benefits, items, or services to or on behalf of individuals or households and is financed in whole or in part with Federal funds, whether administered directly by a Federal agency or through a State, local government, or other entity. The term includes Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security retirement, survivors, and disability insurance, supplemental security income, supplemental nutrition assistance, child nutrition, temporary assistance for needy families, unemployment compensation, child care assistance, and Federal housing assistance. The term includes payments to providers, contractors, grantees, and other entities for administering or furnishing such benefits, items, or services.
- **(2)** *State* The term “State” means each of the several States, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and any territory or possession of the United States.
- **(3)** *State or local official* The term “State or local official” means an elected or appointed officer or employee of a State, a political subdivision of a State, or an agency or instrumentality thereof.
- **(4)** *Federal loss* The term “Federal loss” means the actual pecuniary loss of Federal funds caused by fraud involving a Federal benefit program, excluding any portion borne solely by a State, local government, or private person. The term does not include the value of benefits, items, or services for which payment was lawfully due.

## § 3. Criminal penalties for Federal benefits fraud

- **(a)** *Offense* Chapter 47 of title 18, United States Code, is amended by adding at the end the following:
  > ## § 1041. Federal benefits fraud
  >
  > - **(a)** *Offense* Whoever knowingly executes, or attempts to execute, a scheme or artifice to defraud a Federal benefit program, or to obtain or divert money or property of such a program by means of materially false or fraudulent pretenses, representations, or promises, shall be punished as provided in this section.
  > - **(b)** *Imprisonment*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* A person convicted of an offense under subsection (a) shall be imprisoned for not less than the applicable minimum term of imprisonment specified under paragraph (2) and not more than life, and may be fined under this title.
  >   - **(2)** *Mandatory minimums* The minimum terms of imprisonment specified under this paragraph are the following:
  >     - **(A)** 1 year, if the Federal loss is less than $40,000.
  >     - **(B)** 2 years, if the Federal loss is at least $40,000 but less than $250,000.
  >     - **(C)** 5 years, if the Federal loss is at least $250,000 but less than $1,000,000.
  >     - **(D)** 10 years, if the Federal loss is at least $1,000,000 but less than $10,000,000.
  >     - **(E)** 15 years, if the Federal loss is at least $10,000,000 but less than $100,000,000.
  >     - **(F)** 20 years, if the Federal loss is $100,000,000 or more.
  > - **(c)** *Repeat offenders* If a person commits an offense under subsection (a) after a prior conviction for a felony involving fraud against a Federal benefit program under Federal or State law has become final, the minimum term of imprisonment otherwise applicable under subsection (b) shall be doubled. The maximum term shall remain life imprisonment.
  > - **(d)** *Determination of loss* For purposes of subsections (b) and (c):
  >   - **(1)** *Actual loss* Only actual Federal loss shall be considered to determine the applicable mandatory minimum. An offense consisting of an attempt that causes no actual Federal loss shall be subject to the minimum term of imprisonment under subsection (b)(1).
  >   - **(2)** *Attributable loss* Federal loss attributable to the defendant includes loss caused by the defendant and, in the case of jointly undertaken criminal activity, loss within the scope of that activity, in furtherance of that activity, and reasonably foreseeable to the defendant. Unrelated schemes may not be aggregated solely because they concern the same Federal benefit program.
  >   - **(3)** *Treatment of payments by the defendant* Payments of restitution and recoveries made after detection of the offense shall not reduce the loss used to determine the mandatory minimum.
  >   - **(4)** *Burden of proof* Every fact necessary to establish a mandatory minimum or increase the otherwise applicable statutory maximum, other than the fact of a prior conviction to the extent permitted by the Constitution, shall be charged in the indictment or information and proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the trier of fact, unless admitted by the defendant in a valid guilty plea.
  > - **(e)** *Probation and suspended sentences* Notwithstanding any other provision of law, a court may not place a natural person convicted under this section on probation or suspend the imposition or execution of the term of imprisonment. This subsection does not limit authority to modify a sentence under section 3582(c).
  > - **(f)** *Substantial assistance* Section 3553(e) of this title and rule 35(b) of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure shall apply to a sentence under this section. A reduction under either provision may not substitute probation for imprisonment.
  > - **(g)** *Relationship to other offenses* Nothing in this section limits prosecution under another provision of Federal law.
  > - **(h)** *Mandatory restitution*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* A defendant convicted under this section shall be ordered to provide to the United States, or any State or unit of local government harmed as a result of the offense, restitution equal to the full amount of the actual loss.
  >   - **(2)** *Procedures* An order under this subsection shall be issued and enforced in accordance with sections 3663A and 3664, subject to paragraph (1). No victim may recover more than the victim’s actual compensatory loss through duplicative restitution, civil damages, or other compensation.
  > - **(i)** *Definitions* In this section:
  >   - **(1)** *Federal benefit program* The term “Federal benefit program” means a program established or authorized by Federal law that provides payments, benefits, items, or services to or on behalf of individuals or households and is financed in whole or in part with Federal funds, whether administered directly by a Federal agency or through a State, local government, or other entity. The term includes Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security retirement, survivors, and disability insurance, supplemental security income, supplemental nutrition assistance, child nutrition, temporary assistance for needy families, unemployment compensation, child care assistance, and Federal housing assistance. The term includes payments to providers, contractors, grantees, and other entities for administering or furnishing such benefits, items, or services.
  >   - **(2)** *State* The term “State” means each of the several States, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and any territory or possession of the United States.
  >   - **(3)** *Federal loss* The term “Federal loss” means the actual pecuniary loss of Federal funds caused by fraud involving a Federal benefit program, excluding any portion borne solely by a State, local government, or private person. The term does not include the value of benefits, items, or services for which payment was lawfully due.

## § 4. Personal civil liability of State and local officials

- **(a)** *Liability* A State or local official shall be personally liable to the United States under this section if the United States establishes that—
  - **(1)** fraud involving a Federal benefit program occurred;
  - **(2)** the official had actual knowledge of that fraud;
  - **(3)** the official had an official duty and lawful authority to take reasonable action to prevent, stop, investigate, or refer that fraud, and had a reasonable opportunity to act;
  - **(4)** the official willfully, intentionally, or recklessly failed to take such action; and
  - **(5)** that failure proximately caused an identifiable Federal loss that reasonable action by the official would have prevented.
- **(b)** *Damages* An official liable under subsection (a) shall be liable in the official’s individual capacity for—
  - **(1)** the full amount of Federal loss proximately caused by the official’s failure to act; or
  - **(2)** if that amount exceeds $1,000,000, additional damages determined by the court so that total damages under paragraph (1) and this paragraph do not exceed three times that amount.
- **(c)** *Enforcement* The Attorney General may bring a civil action under this section in an appropriate United States district court.
- **(d)** *Limitations period* An action under this section may not be brought more than 6 years after the actionable failure to act, or more than 3 years after material facts are known or reasonably should have been known by the responsible official of the United States, whichever is later, but in no event more than 10 years after the actionable failure to act.
- **(e)** *Immunity* A claim under this section shall be brought against an official in the official’s individual capacity. Qualified immunity and immunity supplied by State or local law shall not bar an action under this section. Nothing in this section abrogates constitutionally required immunity or authorizes an action against a State itself.
- **(f)** *Reasonable action* In this section, the term “reasonable action” may include a timely referral to an appropriate investigative or prosecutorial authority, preservation of evidence, implementation of lawful payment controls, or another response reasonably suited to the official’s authority and the known fraud. An official who takes reasonable action shall not be liable solely because another authority fails to act or fraud nevertheless continues.
- **(g)** *Relationship to other remedies* This section does not limit the False Claims Act, criminal liability, administrative remedies, or other remedies available under law. Compensatory recoveries for the same Federal loss shall be credited to prevent duplicative compensation, without eliminating separately authorized punitive damages or civil penalties. No private right of action is created by this section.

## § 5. Sentencing guidelines and reporting

- **(a)** *Sentencing guidelines* Not later than 180 days after enactment, the United States Sentencing Commission shall review and, as appropriate, amend the Federal sentencing guidelines and policy statements to implement this Act, consistent with its authority under title 28, United States Code.
- **(b)** *Report* Not later than 1 year after enactment, and annually for 4 years thereafter, the Attorney General shall submit to the Committees on the Judiciary of the House of Representatives and the Senate a report stating the number of prosecutions and convictions under section 1041 of title 18, United States Code, as added by section 3, the sentencing tiers applied, restitution ordered and collected, civil actions brought under section 4, and damages assessed and collected. The report shall not disclose personally identifiable information concerning beneficiaries or information that would compromise an investigation.

## § 6. Rule of construction; severability

- **(a)** *Beneficiary protections* Nothing in this Act may be construed to change program eligibility, authorize withholding benefits from an eligible individual solely because fraud is suspected elsewhere in a program, or make an inadvertent reporting error, good-faith mistake, or mere receipt of an overpayment a criminal offense absent the elements required by section 1041 of title 18, United States Code, as added by section 3.
- **(b)** *Severability* If any provision of this Act, or its application to any person or circumstance, is held invalid, the remaining provisions and their application to other persons and circumstances shall not be affected.
