# S. 5531

> To amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to establish an excise tax on digital advertising, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “In Real Life Act” or the “IRL Act”.

## § 2. Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to strengthen the social fabric that unites the American people to continue our 250-year-old experiment in shared self-governance by—

- **(1)** pushing big technology companies away from business models that profit from screen addiction and cause withdrawal from in-person connection; and
- **(2)** investing in the local civic infrastructure and organizations that link citizens together in common cause.

- **(b)** *Social Capital Fund* Subchapter A of chapter 98 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 is amended by adding at the end the following new section:
  > ## § 9512. Social Capital Fund
  >
  > - **(a)** *Creation of trust fund* There is established in the Treasury of the United States a trust fund to be known as the Social Capital Fund, consisting of such amounts as may be—
  >   - **(1)** appropriated to such Trust Fund as provided in this section, or
  >   - **(2)** credited to such Trust Fund as provided in section 9602(b).
  > - **(b)** *Transfers to trust fund* There are hereby appropriated to the Social Capital Fund amounts equivalent to the taxes received in the Treasury under chapter 50B.
  > - **(c)** *Expenditures from trust fund*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* Subject to paragraph (2), amounts in the Social Capital Fund shall be available to the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development, without further appropriation and to remain available until expended, to make grants for the funding of eligible activities.
  >   - **(2)** *Allocation and distribution of funds* For purposes of making grants under paragraph (1)—
  >     - **(A)** 75 percent of amounts in the Social Capital Fund shall be allocated pursuant to rules similar to the rules under section 106 of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5306) to recipients that—
  >       - **(i)** submit an application described in subsection (e), and
  >       - **(ii)** are eligible for grants authorized by section 103 of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5303), and
  >     - **(B)** 25 percent of amounts in the Social Capital Fund shall be allocated on a competitive basis to recipients that—
  >       - **(i)** submit an application described in subsection (e), and
  >       - **(ii)** are—
  >         - **(I)** a unit of general local government (as defined in section 102(a) of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5302(a))),
  >         - **(II)** an organization described in section 501(c)(3) and exempt from tax under section 501(a),
  >         - **(III)** a religious or civic not-for-profit organization which is at least partially governed, operated, and funded by individuals who are residents of the community served by such organization,
  >         - **(IV)** an Indian tribe (as defined section 102(a) of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5302(a))), or
  >         - **(V)** a consortium of any entities described in subclauses (I) through (IV).
  >   - **(3)** *Nonentitlement areas* With respect to any allocations made pursuant to paragraph (2)(A) to States for use in nonentitlement areas, such amount shall be distributed pursuant to rules similar to the rules under section 106 of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5306).
  > - **(d)** *Eligible activities* For purposes of subsection (c), the term eligible activities means any of the following:
  >   - **(1)** An athletic or other recreational program operated by a governmental, tribal, or not-for-profit organization.
  >   - **(2)** Construction, renovation, operation (including programming), or maintenance of—
  >     - **(A)** parks, trails, playgrounds, recreation centers, pools, libraries, or other public spaces that are accessible at no cost to residents of the community,
  >     - **(B)** museums, arts and cultural centers, or performing arts venues that are affordable for residents of the community, or
  >     - **(C)** beaches that are open and accessible to the public at no cost, including training, supplies, uniforms, and related infrastructure for lifeguards, maintenance staff, and other personnel staffing or maintaining a beach.
  >   - **(3)** Support for any not-for-profit organization that is formed to support any of the spaces and venues described in paragraph (2), such as a friends group.
  >   - **(4)** Support for any Parent Teacher Organization for a public elementary or secondary school.
  >   - **(5)** Programs or services provided by a religious or civic not-for-profit organization which—
  >     - **(A)** is at least partially governed, operated, and funded by individuals who are residents of the community served by such organization, and
  >     - **(B)** certifies that any amounts provided under subsection (c) shall be used to support programs or services which are—
  >       - **(i)** not sectarian worship, instruction, or proselytization, and
  >       - **(ii)** made available to all residents of the community.
  >   - **(6)** Support for locally owned small businesses that serve as social spaces for residents of the community.
  >   - **(7)** Support for festivals or events that are open to the public at no cost.
  >   - **(8)** Disseminating information about local civic life, which may include opportunities to volunteer, organizations to join, or events to attend.
  >   - **(9)** Support for programs to teach responsible use of technology or treat addiction to social media or artificial intelligence.
  >   - **(10)** Support for community development financial institutions (as defined in section 103 of the Community Development Banking and Financial Institutions Act of 1994 (12 U.S.C. 4702)) to expand their financing for any of the activities described in paragraphs (1) through (9).
  >   - **(11)** Community participation, public outreach, administrative costs, and program evaluation for any of the activities described in paragraphs (1) through (10).
  > - **(e)** *Application* An applicant for a grant under subsection (c) shall provide the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development with a detailed proposal for how the grant will help to cultivate civic life, which shall include the following:
  >   - **(1)** The capacity of the applicant to implement its proposal and act as a responsible steward of Federal funds, including through its community partnerships and collaborative networks.
  >   - **(2)** The extent to which the proposal is likely to increase social connection, trust, and participation by people of all incomes and backgrounds in the community.
  >   - **(3)** The extent to which the proposal cultivates organizations that are at least partially governed, operated, and funded by individuals who are residents of the community by involving such organizations in the design and implementation of the proposal, which may include encouraging the creation of new organizations.
  >   - **(4)** Any other information deemed necessary by the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development.
  > - **(f)** *Program evaluation*
  >   - **(1)** *In general* The Secretary of Housing and Urban Development, acting through the Office of Policy Development and Research, shall—
  >     - **(A)** evaluate programs funded by this section, and
  >     - **(B)** disseminate best practices for cultivating civic life.
  >   - **(2)** *Reporting* Any recipient of grant funding under this section shall provide reports and information deemed necessary by the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development to audit or evaluate their program.
  >   - **(3)** *Standardized metrics* Any evaluation under paragraph (1)(A) or report under paragraph (2) shall utilize standardized metrics to minimize administrative and compliance costs, including scientifically validated measurements of evidence-based components of social capital.
