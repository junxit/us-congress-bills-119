# H.R. 11047

> To require certain persons to disclose to the Secretary of Defense covered relationships for covered contracts relating to covered aerospace technology, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Aerospace Transparency Act”.

## § 2. Findings

Congress finds the following:

- **(1)** The People’s Republic of China pursues a national strategy of military-civil fusion, under which the Chinese Communist Party seeks to acquire and direct advanced technology, expertise, and industrial capability developed by nominally civilian and commercial entities toward the modernization of the People’s Liberation Army.
- **(2)** Under this strategy, the distinction between China’s civilian and military industrial bases is deliberately eroded, such that commercial partnerships, technology transfers, and investments involving Chinese enterprises may contribute, directly or indirectly, to the development of military capabilities of the People’s Republic of China.
- **(3)** Aerospace, aviation, and space technologies are among the priority sectors of the military modernization of the People’s Republic of China and among the priority sectors of its national industrial strategy.
- **(4)** The United States Government has formally identified numerous Chinese enterprises as contributors to China’s defense-industrial base or military modernization through multiple designation regimes, including the list of Chinese military companies maintained by the Secretary of Defense pursuant to section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (10 U.S.C. 113 note), the Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List maintained by the Secretary of the Treasury, the Entity List set forth in Supplement No. 4 to part 744 of the Export Administration Regulations, and the Military End User List set forth in Supplement No. 7 to part 744 of the Export Administration Regulations.
- **(5)** These designations reflect a determination by the United States Government that the identified entities present a national security concern by virtue of their role in, or support for, military modernization of the People’s Republic of China.
- **(6)** Documented cases demonstrate that a single Chinese state-owned enterprise may simultaneously serve as a supplier or manufacturing partner to Western commercial aerospace programs and as a principal developer of military aircraft for the People’s Liberation Army, including strategic bombers and military transport aircraft, such that commercial cooperation with that enterprise is not readily separable form its military production.
- **(7)** Advance Western industrial design, simulation, and manufacturing software has been used in the development of fielded combat aircraft of the People’s Liberation Army, demonstrating that commercially available engineering tools can materially contribute to the design and production of foreign military systems.
- **(8)** Western firms have entered into joint ventures, technology-transfer agreements, co-production agreements, and equity investments with Chinese enterprises that the United States Government has designated as contributors to China’s military modernization, or with subsidiaries of such enterprises, including enterprises engaged in the development of military aircraft, missiles, and space systems.
- **(9)** In certain of these cases, the firm engaged in such a relationship holds, directly or through a parent company or an entity under common control, contracts, subcontracts, grants, or other agreements with the Department of Defense.
- **(10)** There is at present no comprehensive requirement that entities holding aerospace-related contracts, subcontracts, grants, or other agreements with the Department of Defense disclose to the Department their relationships, or the relationships of their corporate affiliates, with entities designated by the United States Government as contributors to China’s military modernization.
- **(11)** As a result, the Department of Defense lacks visibility into supply chain, security and capability transfer risks arising from the relationships that its contractors, or the corporate affiliates of its contractors, maintain with such designated entities.
- **(12)** Such visibility is necessary for the Department of Defense to assess and, where warranted, mitigate risks to the integrity of the defense aerospace supply chain and to the security of Department of Defense programs.
- **(13)** The United States retains the authority to establish, as a condition of eligibility for contract, subcontracts, grants, and other agreements with the Department of Defense, requirements that contractors disclose information material to the national security interests of the United States.
- **(14)** Requiring disclosure of relationships with designated entities, while reserving to the Department of Defense the assessment of such relationships and any determination regarding their consequences, is a measured and appropriately limited means of closing the visibility gap described in this section.

## § 3. Disclosure of covered relationships for covered contracts relating to covered aerospace technology

- **(a)** *In general*
  - **(1)** *Disclosure required* Each offeror for a covered contract shall submit to the Secretary of Defense, along with the offer for such covered contract, a disclosure of each covered relationship of the offeror.
  - **(2)** *Guidance* Not later than 90 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of Defense shall issue guidance implementing the disclosure requirements of paragraph (1), including the form, manner, and recipient of required disclosures, which shall identify—
    - **(A)** the covered entity;
    - **(B)** the nature and date of the covered relationship;
    - **(C)** the covered contractor or offeror, including a parent or affiliate of the covered contractor or offeror, that is party to the relationship; and
    - **(D)** such other information as the Secretary may require.
  - **(3)** *Initial disclosure* Not later than 180 days after the date on which the Secretary issues guidance under paragraph (2), a covered contractor shall provide to the Secretary of Defense the disclosures required under paragraph (1) for each covered relationship of the covered contractor that was in effect during the period between the date of the enactment of this Act and the date on which such guidance was issued.
  - **(4)** *Disclosure of new relationships* A covered contractor shall provide to the Secretary of Defense the disclosures required under paragraph (1) for each covered relationship of the covered contractor entered into after the date on which the Secretary issues guidance under paragraph (2) not later than 90 days after entering into such covered relationship.
  - **(5)** *Newly designated entities* A covered contractor shall provide to the Secretary of Defense the disclosures required under paragraph (1) for each relationship between the covered contractor and an entity that becomes a covered entity after the date on which the Secretary issues guidance under paragraph (2) not later than 90 days after the covered contractor determines that such entity is a covered entity.
- **(b)** *Assessment and response to disclosures*
  - **(1)** *Assessment* The Secretary of Defense shall assess each covered relationship disclosed under subsection (a) for security risks, supply chain risks, and risks relating to the transfer of capabilities and may take such action as the Secretary determines appropriate, including—
    - **(A)** requiring mitigation measures; or
    - **(B)** considering the disclosure or failure to disclose as a factor in any determination regarding the renewal or continuation of a covered contract.
  - **(2)** *Rule of construction* Nothing in this subsection shall be construed to require the Secretary of Defense—
    - **(A)** to take any particular action with respect to a disclosed covered relationship; or
    - **(B)** to prohibit an offeror or covered contractor from maintaining a covered relationship disclosed under this section.
- **(c)** *Report*
  - **(1)** *In general* Not later than one year after the Secretary issues guidance under subsection (a)(2), and annually thereafter, the Secretary of Defense shall submit to the congressional defense committees a report that includes the number and general nature of covered relationships disclosed under this section and any actions taken under subsection (b).
  - **(2)** *Form* The report required under this subsection shall be submitted in unclassified form but may contain a classified annex.
- **(d)** *Definitions* In this section:
  - **(1)** The term controlled technology means technology—
    - **(A)** described in or otherwise classified under the Commerce Control List set forth in Supplement No. 1 to part 774 of the Export Administration Regulations; or
    - **(B)** subject to the Export Administration Regulations but not described in or otherwise classified under the Commerce Control List, designated as EAR99.
  - **(2)** The term covered contractor means a contractor performing a covered contract on the date of the enactment of this Act.
  - **(3)** The term covered contract means a contract, grant, or other transaction agreement offered by the Department of Defense for the design, development, production, sustainment, or supply of covered aerospace technology.
  - **(4)** The term covered aerospace technology means software, intellectual property, or technology used in the design, development, simulation, production, or sustainment of aircraft, spacecraft, launch vehicles, propulsion systems, avionics, or related systems and subsystems intended for use by the Department of Defense.
  - **(5)** The term covered relationship means a relationship between an offeror for a covered contract or a covered contractor and an entity that is a covered entity, where the offeror or covered contractor knows or reasonably should have known the entity was a covered entity, and includes—
    - **(A)** a joint venture;
    - **(B)** a transfer or license of technology or intellectual property;
    - **(C)** a co-production arrangement;
    - **(D)** any ownership of stock or shares, including partial ownership; or
    - **(E)** any other arrangement providing recurring access to the offeror’s controlled technology, personnel, or facilities.
  - **(6)** The term covered entity means—
    - **(A)** any entity on—
      - **(i)** the list of Chinese military companies maintained by the Secretary of Defense pursuant to section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (10 U.S.C. 113 note);
      - **(ii)** the Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List maintained by the Secretary of the Treasury;
      - **(iii)** the Entity List set forth in Supplement No. 4 to part 744 of the Export Administration Regulations; or
      - **(iv)** the Military End User List set forth in Supplement No. 7 to part 744 of the Export Administration Regulations; or
    - **(B)** any entity that is majority-owned by an entity described in subparagraph (A).
