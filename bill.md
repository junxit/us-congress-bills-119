# H.R. 10922

> To impose liability on State officers and employees who fail to prevent fraud, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Fraud Accountability and Mandatory Sentencing Act”.

## § 2. Personal liability for State officers and employees

- **(a)** *Prohibition* It shall be unlawful for any officer or employee of a State who has knowledge of fraud in the administration of or use of a covered benefit within that State, and the power to prevent or aid in preventing the continuing commission of the same, to fail or refuse so to do.
- **(b)** *Enforcement* The Attorney General may bring a civil action against an officer or employee who violates subsection (a) in a United States district court for appropriate relief, including—
  - **(1)** in the case of aggregate loss to a governmental entity of $500,000 or less, damages in an amount not exceeding the amount of aggregate loss resulting from such violation; and
  - **(2)** in the case of aggregate loss to a governmental entity exceeding $500,000, damages in an amount not exceeding 3 times the amount of aggregate loss resulting from such violation.
- **(c)** *Immunity* No immunity that would otherwise be available as a result of a defendant acting within the scope of their office or employment at the time of the violation out of which the claim arose shall apply in any action under this section.
- **(d)** *Definitions* In this section:
  - **(1)** *Covered benefit* The term “covered benefit” means any benefit under—
    - **(A)** the supplemental nutrition assistance program established under the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 2011 et seq.);
    - **(B)** the old-age, survivors, and disability insurance program under title II of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 401 et seq.);
    - **(C)** the State unemployment compensation programs receiving funding under title III of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 501 et seq.);
    - **(D)** the income assistance program under title XVI of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1381 et seq.);
    - **(E)** the health insurance program under title XVIII of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1395 et seq.);
    - **(F)** a State plan under title XIX of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1396 et seq.) (or waiver of such plan);
    - **(G)** the program under the Child Care and Development Block Grant Act of 1990 (42 U.S.C. 9857 et seq.); or
    - **(H)** any other program pursuant to which funds are made available to States.
  - **(2)** *Loss*
    - **(A)** *In general* The term “loss” means the greater of actual loss, intended loss, or the gain that resulted from the fraudulent conduct.
    - **(B)** *Actual loss* The term “actual loss” means the reasonably foreseeable pecuniary harm that resulted from the fraudulent conduct.
    - **(C)** *Intended loss* The term “intended loss” means the pecuniary harm that the fraudulent actor purposely sought to inflict, including intended pecuniary harm that would have been impossible or unlikely to occur.
    - **(D)** *Pecuniary harm* The term “pecuniary harm” means harm that is monetary or that otherwise is readily measurable in money.
    - **(E)** *Reasonably foreseeable pecuniary harm* The term “reasonably foreseeable pecuniary harm” means pecuniary harm that the fraudulent actor knew or, under the circumstances, reasonably should have known, was a potential result of the offense.

## § 3. Mandatory minimum sentences

- **(a)** *In general* Subchapter A of chapter 227 of title 18, United States Code, is amended by inserting after section 3559 the following:
  > ## § 3559A. Mandatory minimum sentences for certain fraud offenses
  >
  > - **(a)** *Mandatory minimum* Notwithstanding any other provision of law, a defendant who has been found guilty of a covered fraud offense that involves a covered benefit shall be sentenced as follows with respect to criminal conduct that occurred after the date of enactment of this section:
  >   - **(1)** For any violation involving loss of less than $40,000, not less than 1 year in prison, and not more than life.
  >   - **(2)** For any violation involving loss of $40,000 or more but less than $250,000, not less than 2 years in prison, and not more than life.
  >   - **(3)** For any violation involving loss of $250,000 or more but less than $5 million, not less than 5 years in prison, and not more than life.
  >   - **(4)** For violations involving loss of $5,000,000 or more but less than $50 million, not less than 10 years in prison, and not more than life.
  >   - **(5)** For violations involving loss of $50,000,000 or more but less than $100 million, not less than 15 years in prison, and not more than life.
  >   - **(6)** For violations involving loss of $100,000,000 or more, not less than 20 years in prison, and not more than life.
  > - **(b)** *Successive violation* A defendant who commits a covered fraud offense after the date on which a prior conviction for a covered fraud offence becomes final shall be sentenced to a term of imprisonment of not less than twice the applicable term under subsection (a).
  > - **(c)** *No prohibition* Notwithstanding any other provision of law, a court may not place on probation or suspend the sentence of any person sentenced under this section.
  > - **(d)** *Definitions* In this section:
  >   - **(1)** *Covered benefit* The term “covered benefit” means any benefit under—
  >     - **(A)** the supplemental nutrition assistance program established under the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 2011 et seq.);
  >     - **(B)** the old-age, survivors, and disability insurance program under title II of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 401 et seq.);
  >     - **(C)** the State unemployment compensation programs receiving funding under title III of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 501 et seq.);
  >     - **(D)** the income assistance program under title XVI of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1381 et seq.);
  >     - **(E)** the health insurance program under title XVIII of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1395 et seq.);
  >     - **(F)** a State plan under title XIX of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1396 et seq.) (or waiver of such plan);
  >     - **(G)** the program under the Child Care and Development Block Grant Act of 1990 (42 U.S.C. 9857 et seq.); or
  >     - **(H)** any other program pursuant to which funds are made available to States.
  >   - **(2)** *Covered fraud offense* The term “covered fraud offense” means an offense under—
  >     - **(A)** section 201, 287, 371, 513, 641, 666(a)(1)(A), 1001, 1028, 1029, 1030, 1031, 1040, 1341, 1343, 1344, 1347, 1348, or 1349 of this title;
  >     - **(B)** section 208 or 1632 of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 408 or 1383a);
  >     - **(C)** section 15 of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008 (7 U.S.C. 2024); or
  >     - **(D)** section 6102 of title 38.
  >   - **(3)** *Loss*
  >     - **(A)** *In general* The term “loss” means the greater of actual loss, intended loss, or the gain that resulted from the fraudulent conduct.
  >     - **(B)** *Actual loss* The term “actual loss” means the reasonably foreseeable pecuniary harm that resulted from the fraudulent conduct.
  >     - **(C)** *Intended loss* The term “intended loss” means the pecuniary harm that the fraudulent actor purposely sought to inflict, including intended pecuniary harm that would have been impossible or unlikely to occur.
  >     - **(D)** *Pecuniary harm* The term “pecuniary harm” means harm that is monetary or that otherwise is readily measurable in money.
