# H.R. 10952

> To promote forest health and reduce wildfire risk by prioritizing forest management through stewardship in high-risk firesheds, providing tax incentives for biomass energy production, and supporting economic utilization of forest products, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026”.

## § 2. Findings; sense of Congress

- **(a)** *Findings* Congress finds the following:
  - **(1)** Catastrophic wildfire imposes substantial costs on communities and the public through loss of life and property, exposure to harmful smoke, disruption of economic activity, and damage to natural resources.
  - **(2)** Research examining forest fuels treatments in the western United States indicates that appropriately implemented treatments can reduce wildfire spread and severity and generate expected avoided damages exceeding treatment costs.
  - **(3)** The benefits of effective fuels treatments extend beyond the treated areas and create benefits that landowners, contractors, and commercial users of forest material cannot fully realize through private transactions.
  - **(4)** Collection, processing, storage, and transportation costs for hazardous fuels reduction activities can exceed the commercial value of the material removed via such activities and create an economic barrier to completing such activities and other beneficial treatments.
  - **(5)** Commercial utilization of suitable fuels treatment material can offset otherwise unrecoverable treatment costs and support the expense of the infrastructure, equipment, and skilled workforce necessary to undertake recurring forest health activities.
- **(b)** *Sense of Congress* It is the sense of Congress that—
  - **(1)** timely review and approval of projects related to forest health should be complemented with targeted tax incentives and grants that improve the economics of carrying out effective hazardous fuels management activities in priority firesheds, including by supporting commercially viable utilization of forest products removed through such activities; and
  - **(2)** projects related to forest health should—
    - **(A)** encourage private investment and competition, accommodate appropriate energy and non-energy uses, and prioritize treatments and wildfire risk mitigation activities in areas at a high risk for wildfire; and
    - **(B)** be evaluated according to the likelihood of hazardous fuels treatment completion, expected wildfire risk reduction, net project costs, and any verified utilization of forest products removed through such projects.

## § 3. Designation of fireshed management areas

- **(a)** *Designation of fireshed management areas*
  - **(1)** *Initial designations* For the 5-year period beginning on the date of enactment of this Act, there are designated fireshed management areas, which—
    - **(A)** shall be comprised of individual landscape-scale firesheds identified as being a high-risk fireshed in the “Wildfire Crisis Strategy” published by the Forest Service in January 2022;
    - **(B)** shall be comprised of individual landscape-scale firesheds identified by the Secretary of Agriculture, in consultation with the Secretary of the Interior, as being in the top 20 percent of the 7,688 firesheds published by the Rocky Mountain Research Station of the Forest Service in 2019 for wildfire exposure based on—
      - **(i)** wildfire exposure and corresponding risk to communities, including risk to life and structures;
      - **(ii)** wildfire exposure and corresponding risk to municipal watersheds, including tribal water supplies and systems; and
      - **(iii)** risk of forest conversion due to wildfire;
    - **(C)** shall not overlap with any other fireshed management areas; and
    - **(D)** may contain Federal and non-Federal land, including Indian forest lands or rangelands.
  - **(2)** *Further fireshed management area designations*
    - **(A)** *In general* On the date that is 5 years after the date of the enactment of this Act, and every 5 years thereafter, the Secretary, in consultation with the Secretary of the Interior, shall submit to the relevant Congressional Committees an updated map of firesheds based on the Fireshed Registry maintained under subsection (c).
    - **(B)** *Designation* Not later than 60 days after submitting an updated fireshed map under subparagraph (A), the Secretary shall, based on such map, designate additional fireshed management areas that are identified as being in the top 20 percent of firesheds at risk of wildfire exposure based on the criteria specified in subparagraphs (B) through (D) of paragraph (1).
- **(b)** *Required activities* In each fireshed management area designated under subsection (a)—
  - **(1)** with respect to National Forest System lands within such areas, the Secretary of Agriculture shall establish and implement a long-term stewardship management strategy that prioritizes the use of contracts and agreements authorized under section 604 of the Healthy Forests Restoration Act of 2003 (16 U.S.C. 6591c) to carry out hazardous fuels reduction and forest restoration projects, including the commercially viable utilization of forest products removed through such projects; and
  - **(2)** with respect to Federal lands within such areas that are not National Forest System lands, the Secretary with jurisdiction over such lands shall carry out hazardous fuels reduction, forest restoration, and other wildfire risk mitigation activities, as appropriate, in coordination with the Secretary of Agriculture and State, local, and Tribal governments, and private landowners, as applicable.
- **(c)** *Fireshed registry* The Secretary shall maintain a Fireshed Registry on a publicly accessible website that provides interactive geospatial data on individual firesheds, including information on—
  - **(1)** wildfire exposure delineated by ownership, including rights-of-way for utilities and other public or private purposes;
  - **(2)** any hazardous fuels management activities that have occurred within an individual fireshed in the past 10 years;
  - **(3)** wildfire exposure with respect to such fireshed delineated by—
    - **(A)** wildfire exposure and corresponding risk to communities, including risk to life and structures;
    - **(B)** wildfire exposure and corresponding risk to municipal watersheds, including tribal water supplies and systems; and
    - **(C)** risk of forest conversion due to wildfire;
  - **(4)** the percentage of the fireshed that has burned in wildfires in the past 10 years, including, to the extent practicable, delineations of acres that have burned at a high severity;
  - **(5)** spatial patterns of wildfire exposure, including plausible extreme fire events; and
  - **(6)** any hazardous fuels management activities planned for the fireshed, including fireshed management projects.
- **(d)** *Applicability of NEPA* The designation of fireshed management areas under this section shall not be subject to the requirements of the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.).
- **(e)** *Definitions* In this section:
  - **(1)** *Fireshed* The term fireshed means a landscape-scale area that faces similar wildfire threat where a response strategy could influence the wildfire outcome.
  - **(2)** *Hazardous fuels management activities* The term hazardous fuels management activities means any vegetation management activities (or combination thereof) that reduce the risk of wildfire, including mechanical thinning, mastication, prescribed burning, cultural burning (as determined by the applicable Indian Tribe), timber harvest, and grazing.
  - **(3)** *Indian forest land or rangeland* The term Indian forest land or rangeland means land that—
    - **(A)** is held in trust by, or with a restriction against alienation by, the United States for an Indian Tribe or a member of an Indian Tribe; and
    - **(B)**
      - **(i)**
        - **(I)** is Indian forest land (as defined in section 304 of the National Indian Forest Resources Management Act (25 U.S.C. 3103)); and
        - **(II)** has a cover of grasses, brush, or any similar vegetation; or
      - **(ii)** formerly had a forest cover or vegetative cover that is capable of restoration.
  - **(4)** *Indian Tribe* The term Indian Tribe has the meaning given that term in section 4 of the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (25 U.S.C. 5304).
  - **(5)** *National Forest System* The term National Forest System has the meaning given the term in section 11(a) of the Forest and Rangeland Renewable Resources Planning Act of 1974 (16 U.S.C. 1609).
  - **(6)** *Public lands* The term public lands has the meaning given that term in section 103 of the Federal Land Policy and Management Act of 1976 (43 U.S.C. 1702), except that the term includes Coos Bay Wagon Road Grant lands and Oregon and California Railroad Grant lands.
  - **(7)** *Relevant Congressional Committees* The term relevant Congressional Committees means—
    - **(A)** the Committees on Natural Resources and Agriculture of the House of Representatives; and
    - **(B)** the Committees on Energy and Natural Resources and Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry of the Senate.
  - **(8)** *State* The term State means each of the several States, the District of Columbia, and each territory of the United States.

## § 4. Reinstatement of investment and production credits for open- and closed-loop biomass facilities

- **(a)** *Investment credit* Section 48(a) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 is amended—
  - **(1)** by redesignating paragraph (16) as paragraph (17), and
  - **(2)** by inserting after paragraph (15) the following new paragraph:
    > - **(16)** *Application of credit to certain biomass facilities*
    >   - **(A)** *In general* In the case of any qualified property (as defined in paragraph (5)(D)) which is part of a specified biomass facility—
    >     - **(i)** such property shall be treated as energy property for purposes of this section, and
    >     - **(ii)** the energy percentage with respect to such property is 30 percent.
    >   - **(B)** *Specified biomass facility* For purposes of this paragraph, the term specified biomass facility means any facility—
    >     - **(i)** described in paragraph (2) or (3) of section 45(d);
    >     - **(ii)** the construction of which begins after the date of the enactment of the Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026;
    >     - **(iii)** located within, or not more than 100 miles from, the boundary of a fireshed management area designated under section 3 of the Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026; and
    >     - **(iv)** certified by the Secretary of Agriculture, in consultation with the Secretary of the Interior, as a facility reasonably expected to obtain not less than 50 percent of biomass feedstock processed through the facility annually, measured by energy content, from forest products removed through hazardous fuels reduction activities carried out in 1 or more such fireshed management areas.

- **(b)** *Production credit*
  - **(1)** *Application to new biomass facilities* Section 45(d) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 is amended—
    - **(A)** in paragraphs (2) and (3), by inserting “or after the date of the enactment of the Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026,” after “January 1, 2025,” each place it appears, and
    - **(B)** in paragraph (3)(A)(ii), by inserting “, or after the date of the enactment of the Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026” after “January 1, 2025”.
  - **(2)** *Termination of certain limitations* Section 45(b)(4) of such Code is amended by adding at the end the following new subparagraph:
    > - **(C)** *Termination* Subparagraphs (A) and (B) shall not apply to any facility described in paragraph (2) or (3) of subsection (d) the construction of which begins after the date of the enactment of the Health Forests Incentives Act of 2026.

- **(c)** *Effective date* The amendments made by this section shall apply to facilities the construction of which begins after the date of the enactment of this Act.

## § 7. Government Accountability Office study on Federal forest restoration and wildfire management

- **(a)** *Study required* The Comptroller General of the United States shall conduct a study to determine—
  - **(1)** the effectiveness of stewardship contracts and agreements authorized under section 604 of the Healthy Forests Restoration Act of 2003 (16 U.S.C. 6591c) in achieving forest restoration, hazardous fuels reduction, and wildfire risk mitigation objectives, including—
    - **(A)** the performance of any such contracts and agreements funded in whole or in part by the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (Public Law 117–58; 135 Stat. 429) or the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (Public Law 117–169; 136 Stat. 1818), including an assessment of expenditures, administrative costs, completed treatments, and any other factors contributing to differences in performance among entities carrying out such contracts and agreements;
    - **(B)** the accuracy and reliability of performance measures the Forest Service uses for timber production, hazardous fuels reduction, and forest restoration on National Forest Service lands, including whether reported accomplishments under such performance measures involve any double counting of timber volumes, treatment acres, or any other outputs;
    - **(C)** the institutional capacity and management practices of the Forest Service to develop, administer, and execute effectively such contracts and agreements, including accounting for the technical and commercial expertise of the Forest Service, relationships between the Forest Service and the forest products industry, and the ability of the Forest Service to achieve wildfire risk reduction and forest restoration objectives on lands subject to such contracts or agreements; and
    - **(D)** identification of any statutory, administrative, financial, or market barriers that impede the timely and cost-effective implementation of such contracts or agreements, including implementation across Federal and non-Federal land ownership boundaries;
  - **(2)** the relationship between wildfire risk mitigation activities carried out by each the Secretary of Agriculture and the Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency, including—
    - **(A)** the extent to which the Secretary and the Administrator, respectively, prioritize non-emergency hazardous fuels reduction activities and non-emergency forest restoration activities over emergency wildfire response and post-disaster mitigation when operating Federal programs subject to the jurisdiction of, and expending funds made available to, the Secretary and the Administrator, respectively;
    - **(B)** the extent to which emergency wildfire suppression activities, including the use of backfiring and burnout operations, and post-disaster grants issued for use removing hazardous fuels supplement or substitute for non-emergency forest management activities; and
    - **(C)** a determination by the Comptroller General as to whether existing Federal authorities, funding mechanisms, and institutional incentives applicable to the Secretary and the Administrator, respectively, promote a coordinated, long-term approach to wildfire risk reduction over reliance on emergency wildfire response and post-disaster mitigation; and
  - **(3)** the extent to which changes to any Federal program, rule, policy, practice, or authority applicable to activities and objectives described in paragraphs (1) and (2) are likely to—
    - **(A)** increase the capacity of the Federal Government and partners of agencies therein to carry out hazardous fuels reduction activities and forest restoration activities;
    - **(B)** improve the effectiveness, transparency, and accountability of the expenditure of funds made available to the Federal Government for wildfire mitigation activities and forest restoration activities;
    - **(C)** facilitate sustained, landscape-scale restoration activities on Federal lands and the commercially viable utilization of forest products removed through such activities; and
    - **(D)** improve community protection from wildfire, mitigate wildfire risk, and prevent the loss of structures and other personal property due to wildfire.
- **(b)** *Report* Not later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Comptroller General shall submit to the Committees on Natural Resources and Agriculture of the House of Representatives and the Committees on Energy and Natural Resources and Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry of the Senate a report containing the results of the study under subsection (a) and any recommendations of the Comptroller General to improve the reliability and efficacy of Federal forest restoration activities, hazardous fuels reduction activities, and timber production performance measures and programs.
- **(c)** *National Forest System defined* In this section, the term National Forest System has the meaning given the term in section 11(a) of the Forest and Rangeland Renewable Resources Planning Act of 1974 (16 U.S.C. 1609).
