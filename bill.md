# H.R. 10673

> To require an external placard to be displayed on containers, vehicles, and rail cars transporting lithium-ion batteries, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Battery Safety Placarding Act”.

## § 2. Placarding requirement for transportation of lithium-ion batteries

- **(a)** *Issuance of rule*
  - **(1)** *In general* Not later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of Transportation shall revise the placarding requirements under subpart F of part 172 of title 49, Code of Federal Regulations, to require an external placard to be displayed on any freight container, unit load device, transport vehicle, or rail car transporting a certain quantity of lithium-ion batteries.
  - **(2)** *Requirements* The Secretary shall—
    - **(A)** establish the quantity of lithium-ion batteries subject to the placarding requirements revised under this subsection based on physical characteristics of the batteries, including weight and dimensions, and energy capacity, and in a manner that accounts for the diverse risk profiles across the transportation lifecycle of different lithium-ion battery products; and
    - **(B)** require that a placard described in paragraph (1)—
      - **(i)** be clearly visible from all sides of the freight container, unit load device, transport vehicle, or rail car; and
      - **(ii)** be identifiable by law enforcement and first responders.
  - **(3)** *Economic impact assessment* In revising the regulations required under this subsection, the Secretary shall—
    - **(A)** conduct an economic impact assessment to evaluate the costs and effects of compliance with such revisions, including—
      - **(i)** placard manufacturing costs;
      - **(ii)** supply chain disruptions; and
      - **(iii)** effects on small and rural businesses and transportation providers; and
    - **(B)** account for any findings from such assessment in such revisions.
- **(b)** *Consultation* In carrying out subsection (a), the Secretary shall consult with the following stakeholders:
  - **(1)** Representatives of Federal, State, local, and Tribal law enforcement and first responder agencies.
  - **(2)** Representatives of affected modes of transportation (including motor carriers, rail carriers, air carriers, maritime carriers, freight forwarders), logistics providers, and associated trade associations.
  - **(3)** Supply chain experts.
  - **(4)** Hazardous materials transportation experts.
  - **(5)** Stakeholders with expertise in the manufacture, distribution, and sale of lithium-ion batteries and products containing such batteries.
  - **(6)** Other appropriate Federal or State agencies and stakeholders.
- **(c)** *Report to Congress* Not later than 30 days after the Secretary revises the regulations required under subsection (a), the Secretary shall brief Congress on such revisions.
- **(d)** *Periodic review* After revising the regulations required under subsection (a), the Secretary shall—
  - **(1)** not less frequently than biennially, review the quantity thresholds established under subsection (a)(2)(A);
  - **(2)** periodically review the effectiveness of the revised placarding requirements for lithium-ion batteries and new technologies and emergency response capabilities; and
  - **(3)** if the Secretary determines that any modification of such regulations are necessary as a result of such review, the Secretary shall submit to Congress a report before implementing any modification.
