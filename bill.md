# H.R. 10854

> To direct the Secretary of Energy to establish and carry out a Public Safety Power Shutoff Event Reporting Program.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Public Safety Power Shutoff Data Transparency Act”.

## § 2. Public safety power shutoff event reporting program

- **(a)** *Establishment* The Secretary of Energy shall establish and carry out a program, to be known as the “Public Safety Power Shutoff Event Reporting Program”, within the Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response.
- **(b)** *Reporting requirements* In carrying out the program, the Secretary shall require each covered electric utility to, not later than 7 days after initiating, and not later than 7 days after ending, a public safety power shutoff event, report information regarding the public safety power shutoff event to the Secretary, including—
  - **(1)** the geographic extent (county or census-tract scale) of the public safety power shutoff event;
  - **(2)** the start or end time, as applicable, for the public safety power shutoff event;
  - **(3)** the estimated number of customers and the critical services affected by the public safety power shutoff event;
  - **(4)** the hazard conditions prompting the public safety power shutoff event;
  - **(5)** a summary of public communications made to customers regarding the public safety power shutoff event;
  - **(6)** a summary of any coordination regarding the public safety power shutoff event with State, local, Tribal, and emergency-response entities;
  - **(7)** a summary of advance notifications provided to, and consultation conducted with, hospitals and other critical health infrastructure within the impacted area prior to and during the public safety power shutoff event; and
  - **(8)** where practicable, the estimated impact of the public safety power shutoff event on underserved or infrastructure-vulnerable communities, including rural, low-income, and Tribal communities.
- **(c)** *Format and integration* In carrying out the program, the Secretary may incorporate fields for public safety power shutoff events into existing outage-reporting mechanisms (such as Form OE–417) to avoid duplication and reduce reporting burden.
- **(d)** *Aggregated transparency and community access*
  - **(1)** *Summaries available to the public* In carrying out the program, the Secretary shall, subject to paragraph (4), publish, on a quarterly basis, on a publicly accessible website of the Department of Energy, anonymized, aggregated summaries of the information reported to the Secretary under this section.
  - **(2)** *Inclusions* Summaries published under paragraph (1) shall include—
    - **(A)** information on frequency, duration, and regional trends for public safety power shutoff events; and
    - **(B)** where available, include data on effects of public safety power shutoff events in rural, low-income, and Tribal areas.
  - **(3)** *Community notification integration* The Secretary shall make summaries published under paragraph (1) available, in a machine-readable format, to State and local emergency-management agencies for use in improving public alerting and preparedness.
  - **(4)** *Delay in publication* The Secretary may delay or withhold publication of summaries under this subsection as the Secretary determines necessary to protect security or other sensitive information.
  - **(5)** *Community reporting* The Secretary shall provide a mechanism for local governments and Tribal entities to submit verified feedback or supplemental data on the impacts of public safety power shutoff events to inform summaries published under paragraph (1) and best practices and nonbinding guidance issued under paragraph (6).
  - **(6)** *Voluntary risk analysis* The Secretary may review and analyze the information reported and feedback submitted under this section to identify emerging patterns and best practices and issue nonbinding guidance on reducing the impacts of public safety power shutoff events through planning, backup power, and coordination.
- **(e)** *Interagency and state coordination* In carrying out the program, the Secretary shall, subject to subsection (f), share public safety power shutoff information reported to the Secretary, and summaries published, under this section with relevant Federal and State entities, including—
  - **(1)** the Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency;
  - **(2)** the Secretary of Health and Human Services;
  - **(3)** the Chair of the Federal Communications Commission;
  - **(4)** the National Interagency Fire Center; and
  - **(5)** State regulatory authorities, State energy offices, and State emergency management or public safety authorities, hospitals, and emergency medical services networks.
- **(f)** *Protection of information*
  - **(1)** *Safe harbor* Notwithstanding any other provision of law, and subject to paragraph (2), information reported to the Secretary under subsection (a)—
    - **(A)** shall be exempt from disclosure under section 552 of title 5, United States Code (commonly referred to as the Freedom of Information Act);
    - **(B)** shall not, without the written consent of the covered electric utility submitting such information, be used directly by the Secretary, any other Federal, State, or local authority, or any third party, in any civil action arising under Federal or State law if such information is submitted in good faith;
    - **(C)** shall not, without the written consent of the covered electric utility submitting such information, be used or disclosed by any officer or employee of the United States for purposes other than for emergency preparedness or public safety coordination purposes, except—
      - **(i)** in furtherance of an investigation or the prosecution of a criminal act; or
      - **(ii)** when disclosure of the information would be—
        - **(I)** to either House of Congress, or to the extent of matter within its jurisdiction, any committee or subcommittee thereof, any joint committee thereof or subcommittee of any such joint committee; or
        - **(II)** to the Comptroller General, or any authorized representative of the Comptroller General, in the course of the performance of the duties of the General Accounting Office;
    - **(D)** shall not, if provided to a State or local government or government agency—
      - **(i)** be made available pursuant to any State or local law requiring disclosure of information or records;
      - **(ii)** otherwise be disclosed or distributed to any party by said State or local government or government agency without the written consent of the covered electric utility submitting such information; or
      - **(iii)** be used other than for emergency preparedness or public safety coordination purposes; and
    - **(E)** does not constitute a waiver of any applicable privilege or protection provided under law, such as trade secret protection.
  - **(2)** *Applicability* Paragraph (1) does not apply with respect to summaries published under subsection (d).
- **(g)** *Enforcement* Any failure to report information under this section shall be considered a failure to make information available under subsection (b) of section 13 of the Federal Energy Administration Act of 1974 (15 U.S.C. 772(b)).
- **(h)** *Implementation* Not later than 12 months after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall issue regulations to carry out this section. Such regulations shall include requirements and guidance relating to digital-submission protocols, reporting thresholds, and privacy standards.
- **(i)** *Definitions* In this Act:
  - **(1)** *Covered electric utility* The term covered electric utility means—
    - **(A)** an electric utility that serves 25,000 or more customers (as determined by the Secretary using data reported to the Energy Information Administration);
    - **(B)** an electric utility that serves less than 25,000 customers (as determined by the Secretary using data reported to the Energy Information Administration) and conducts a public safety power shutoff event within an area designated by the Secretary as high-risk for wildfire or related natural-hazard conditions, consistent with existing Federal hazard mitigation maps and other relevant Federal or State data sources; or
    - **(C)** any other entity that owns or operates electric distribution facilities and conducts a public safety power shutoff event.
  - **(2)** *Electric utility* The term electric utility has the meaning given such term in section 3 of the Federal Power Act (16 U.S.C. 796).
  - **(3)** *Program* The term program means the Public Safety Power Shutoff Event Reporting Program established under subsection (a).
  - **(4)** *Public safety power shutoff event* The term public safety power shutoff event means a temporary de-energization of electric service to specific areas, initiated by a covered electric utility, and undertaken as a preventive measure to reduce the risk of wildfire, including during extreme weather, or other imminent public safety hazards arising from electric infrastructure.
  - **(5)** *Secretary* The term Secretary means the Secretary of Energy.
