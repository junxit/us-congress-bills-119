# H.R. 11060

> To amend title 18, United States Code, to provide for the effect of a pardon on an order of restitution, and for other purposes.

## § 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the “Pardon Accountability and Victim Protection Act”.

## § 2. Effect of pardon on restitution

- **(a)** *In general* Subchapter B of chapter 229 of title 18, United States Code, is amended by adding at the end the following:
  > ## § 3616. Effect of pardon on restitution
  >
  > - **(a)** *Civil action authorized for victims* A victim may bring an action in an appropriate district court of the United States against a covered pardon recipient seeking the payment of restitution that was ordered to be paid to the victim and has not been paid.
  > - **(b)** *Burden of proof* A victim who brings a civil action under subsection (a) shall have the burden of proving, by a preponderance of the evidence, that—
  >   - **(1)** the defendant is a covered pardon recipient;
  >   - **(2)** the defendant caused the injury to the victim, with respect to which the defendant was ordered to pay restitution to the victim; and
  >   - **(3)** the victim has not been paid the portion of the restitution that the victim seeks in the civil action.
  > - **(c)** *Admissibility of conviction and trial record* Notwithstanding any other provision of law, with respect to the offense for which an individual was pardoned, the court may admit into evidence in an action under this subsection the following:
  >   - **(1)** The order of conviction.
  >   - **(2)** The order of restitution.
  >   - **(3)** The order of clemency.
  >   - **(4)** The record of the trial, plea hearing, and sentencing proceeding, as applicable.
  > - **(d)** *Period for bringing action* An action under this subsection may be brought during the period beginning on the date on which the pardon described in subsection (a) is accepted, and ending on the date that is 5 years after such date.
  > - **(e)** *Covered pardon recipient* In this subsection, the term covered pardon recipient means an individual who—
  >   - **(1)** was convicted of an offense, with respect to which the individual was ordered to pay restitution to a victim;
  >   - **(2)** is granted a pardon for such offense; and
  >   - **(3)** has not paid all of the restitution described in paragraph (1).
