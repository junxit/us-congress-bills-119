# H.R. 10421

> To amend the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 to increase support for elementary and secondary education, and for other purposes.

## § 2. Definitions

In this Act:

- **(1)** *ESEA terms*
  - **(A)** The terms child with a disability, Department, early childhood education program, educational service agency, elementary school, English learner, evidence-based, local educational agency, other staff, outlying area, parent, professional development, school leader, secondary school, specialized instructional support personnel, and State educational agency have the meanings given the terms in section 8101 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7801).
  - **(B)** The term innovative assessment system has the meaning given such term in section 1204(a) of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 6364(a)).
  - **(C)** The term magnet school has the meaning given such term in section 4402 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7231a).
  - **(D)** The term school-based mental health services provider has the meaning given such term in section 4102 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7112).
- **(2)** *Paraprofessional* The term paraprofessional has the meaning given such term in section 8101 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7801), unless otherwise specified.
- **(3)** *Secretary* The term Secretary has the meaning given such term in section 8101 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7801), unless otherwise specified.
- **(4)** *State* The term State has the meaning given such term in section 8101 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 7801), unless otherwise specified.

## § 3. Purposes

The purposes of this Act are to:

- **(1)** Fully fund the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (20 U.S.C. 1400 et seq.).
- **(2)** Support States in providing all students with a well-rounded education, including instruction in the arts, humanities, and social sciences.
- **(3)** Reduce State and Federal reliance on standardized test scores as an indicator of student ability and school quality.
- **(4)** Promote innovative, holistic, and high-quality assessment models that more fully capture student knowledge, skills, and competencies.
- **(5)** Prevent discrimination and bias in K–12 schools and uphold the civil rights of students, staff, and educators.
- **(6)** Rebuild, repair, and restore K–12 public school infrastructure.
- **(7)** Strengthen the educator pipeline and provide educators and staff with the resources they need to succeed in and out of the classroom.
- **(8)** Promote high-quality education research and development and uplift evidence-based practices for student success and wellbeing.
- **(9)** Provide K–12 public schools with the amount of funding necessary to equip all students with the opportunity to achieve challenging academic standards and succeed in college and career.
- **(10)** Provide all young children with access to a free, high-quality preschool education.
- **(11)** Provide all students with free and healthy school meals.
- **(12)** Strengthen and protect public education as a vital community-based institution.
